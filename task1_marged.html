1. The Foundation: Clauses

Before building sentences, understand the building blocks.

1.1 What is a Clause?

A clause is a group of words containing a subject and a verb.

Clause TypeDefinitionCan Stand Alone?Example
Independent ClauseComplete thought✅ YesCar sales increased.
Dependent ClauseIncomplete thought❌ No...while car sales increased...

1.2 Independent Clause (Main Clause)

Formula: [Subject] + [Verb] + (Object/Complement)

Examples Car sales increased.
The figure for London was the highest.
Renewable energy accounted for 30% of total production.

1.3 Dependent Clause (Subordinate Clause)

Begins with a subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun. Cannot stand alone.

TypeSubordinators
Contrastwhile, whereas, although, even though
Timebefore, after, when, as, once, until
Conditionif, unless, provided that
Relativewhich, that, who, whose, where

1.4 Relative Clause

A relative clause is a type of dependent clause that describes or gives information about a noun.

PronounUsed ForFormulaExample (IELTS Task 1)
whoPeople (subject)[Person] + who + [Verb] + ...The proportion of people who lived in urban areas doubled.
whomPeople (object)[Person] + whom + [Subject] + [Verb]The employees whom the company hired in 2015 were mostly graduates.
whosePeople/Things (possession)[Noun] + whose + [Possessed Noun] + [Verb]Germany, whose economy grew fastest, led the group.
whichThings/Animals/Ideas[Thing] + which + [Verb] OR , which + [Data]Sales reached 50,000, which was a record high.
thatPeople/Things (defining only)[Noun] + that + [Subject/Verb]The energy that was generated by coal fell sharply.
wherePlaces/Locations/Levels[Place] + where + [Subject] + [Verb]The figure fell to 3%, where it remained stable.
whenTime[Time] + when + [Subject] + [Verb]The decade when internet usage surged was the 1990s.
whyReason[Reason] + why + [Clause]The reasons why prices increased were complex.

2. Simple Sentences

Definition: A simple sentence contains exactly one independent clause – one subject and one main verb.

Formula: [Subject] + [Verb] + (Object/Complement)

2.2 Five Core Simple Sentence Structures for Task 1

StructureFormulaTask 1 Example
1. Trend Structure[Subject] + [Intransitive Verb] + (Adverb) + (Time/Data)The price of residential property rose sharply between 2005 and 2010.
2. Introduction Structure[Subject] + [Transitive Verb] + [Direct Object]The provided pie chart illustrates the primary modes of transportation in London.
3. Fact/State Structure[Subject] + [Linking Verb] + [Subject Complement]Coal remained the primary source of energy generation until 2015.
4. Compound Subject[Subject 1] + and + [Subject 2] + [Verb]Both commercial shipping and domestic aviation experienced a noticeable decline.
5. Compound Predicate[Subject] + [Verb 1] + and/but + [Verb 2]The unemployment rate peaked at 12% in 2008 but dropped steadily thereafter.
6. In [Year]In + Past Year + , + subject + past tense verb + value.In 2008, Organization A stood at 0.5 billion dollars.

2.3 Loading Simple Sentences with Prepositions

Layer AddedSentence

+ How muchSales in the retail sector increased by 15%.

✅ When to Use Simple Sentences DO NOT avoid simple sentences thinking they are "too basic." They are ideal for overview statements, key comparisons, and introduction sentences.

3. Compound Sentences

Definition: A compound sentence joins two independent clauses of equal importance.

Formula: [Clause 1] + , + [connection] + [Clause 2]  or  [Independent Clause 1] + ; + [Independent Clause 2]

3.2 Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

ConjunctionFunctionTask 1 Use
ForReasonRare in Task 1
AndAddition/Parallel✅ Very Common
NorNegative additionRare
ButContrast✅ Very Common
OrAlternativeRare
YetContrast (formal)✅ Common
SoResult/Consequence✅ Common

3.3 Four Primary Compound Structures

The "And" Structure – Parallel Trends Car production increased by 20% in 2010, and truck manufacturing followed a similar upward trajectory.

In 2010, car production increased by 20%, and truck manufacturing followed a similar upward trajectory.

The first step involves sorting the materials, and the second phase requires crushing them into smaller pieces.
The "But / Yet" Structure – Direct Contrast Coal remained the primary energy source in 1990, but it was overtaken by natural gas in 2005.

Desktop computer sales fell significantly, yet tablet sales remained relatively stable.
The "So" Structure – Result/Consequence The liquid is heated to 100 degrees Celsius, so it evaporates into a gas.

Funding for the project was cut in half, so the construction timeline was delayed.
The Semicolon – "Naked" Connection In 2000, Japan exported 50 million vehicles; Germany exported just 30 million.

Male employment rates peaked in the summer; female employment rates hit their lowest point during the same period.

3.4 Advanced: Semicolon + Conjunctive Adverb

Formula: [Clause 1] + ; + [Transition Word] + , + [Clause 2]

Transition WordFunctionExample
however, in contrast, on the other handContrastProfits grew steadily; however, Company B experienced severe fluctuations.
furthermore, moreoverAdditionSolar energy saw a 10% increase; furthermore, installation costs dropped.
therefore, consequentlyResultThe population doubled; consequently, new infrastructure was built.
❌ The "Comma Splice" Trap Incorrect: Beef consumption fell to 20 pounds, chicken consumption rose to 50 pounds. (Using a comma without a conjunction = Grammatical Error)
✅ Correction 1 (Conjunction): Beef consumption fell to 20 pounds, and chicken consumption rose to 50 pounds.
✅ Correction 2 (Semicolon): Beef consumption fell to 20 pounds; chicken consumption rose to 50 pounds.
✅ Correction 3 (Transition): Beef consumption fell to 20 pounds; however, chicken consumption rose to 50 pounds.

4. Complex Sentences

Definition: A complex sentence connects one independent clause with one or more dependent clauses using a subordinating conjunction.

Formula: [Subordinating Conjunction] + [Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause]  or  [Independent Clause] + , + [Subordinating Conjunction] + [Dependent Clause]

4.2 Contrast Structures

While / Whereas (Direct Comparison) Formula A: [While/Whereas + Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause].
Formula B: [Independent Clause] + , + [while/whereas + Dependent Clause].

While oil consumption dropped to 20 million barrels in 2015, natural gas usage grew to 50 million barrels.
The UK invested heavily in renewable energy, whereas France focused primarily on nuclear power.
Although / Even Though (Surprising Contrast) Formula: [Although + Clause 1] , [Clause 2].

Although coal was the primary energy source in 1990, it was heavily outpaced by natural gas by 2010.
Even though profits dipped in the second quarter, the company ended the year at a record high.

4.3 Time Sequence Structures

The "As" Pattern (Simultaneous Action) Formula: [As + Dependent Clause] , [Independent Clause].

As the temperature of the water rises, the ice melts rapidly.
As the unemployment rate increased, consumer spending decreased proportionately.
The "Before / After" Pattern (Sequential Events) Formula: [Clause 1] + before/after + [Verb-ing] + [Data].

Sales peaked at 500 units in 2015 before dropping sharply the following year.
After remaining stable for an entire decade, the population surged by 20% in 2020.
Once the materials are sorted by hand, they are placed onto a conveyor belt.

4.4 Condition Structures (Processes)

Formula: [If/Unless + Dependent Clause] , [Independent Clause].

If the mixture is not heated to exactly 100°C, the chemical reaction fails.
Unless the raw materials are crushed into a fine powder, they cannot be mixed with water.
⚠️ The Golden Punctuation Rule Dependent Clause FIRST: Comma after dependent clause  →  While profits rose, expenses fell.
Independent Clause FIRST: Usually NO comma  →  Expenses fell while profits rose.
Exception: Comma for "while/whereas" contrast  →  Expenses fell, while profits rose.

5. Compound-Complex Sentences

Definition: A compound-complex sentence contains at least two independent clauses (compound) AND at least one dependent clause (complex).

Formula: [Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause 1] + , and/but + [Independent Clause 2]

5.2 Three Essential Structures

Structure 1: The "Contrast + Addition" Formula: While/Although + [Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause 1] + , and/but + [Independent Clause 2].

While the number of male graduates dropped significantly, female graduate numbers rose to 50,000, and the figure for postgraduates remained perfectly stable.
Although housing accounted for the largest share of the budget in 2000, it was overtaken by food in 2010, but transportation costs stayed consistently low across both years.
Structure 2: The "Compound + Relative" Formula: [Independent Clause 1] + , but/and + [Independent Clause 2] + , which + [Relative Clause].

Coal consumption fell steadily throughout the decade, but natural gas usage surged to 40%, which was the highest figure recorded on the chart.
The UK spent $50 million on healthcare, and France spent a nearly identical amount, which represented a massive increase from the previous year.
Structure 3: The "Compound + Time Sequence" Formula: [Independent Clause 1] + , and + [Independent Clause 2] + before/after + [Dependent Time Clause].

Profits hit a peak of $1 million in 2015, and they remained at this level for exactly three years before dropping sharply in 2019.
The liquid is heated to 100 degrees, and it is passed through a filtration screen before it is bottled for distribution.
⚠️ The "Spaghetti Sentence" Trap NEVER EXCEED 3 CLAUSES IN A SINGLE SENTENCE (1 Independent + 2 Dependent = Maximum)
❌ While car sales dropped, truck sales rose to 50,000, and bus sales remained stable, which was a surprise because fuel prices were rising. (5 clauses = Confusing!)
✅ While car sales dropped, truck sales rose to 50,000, and bus sales remained stable. (3 clauses = Clear, academic, and highly advanced)

6. Relative Clauses Mastery

6.1 Defining vs. Non-Defining

FeatureDefining Relative ClauseNon-Defining Relative Clause
PurposeEssential – identifies which nounExtra – adds background detail
Commas❌ NO commas✅ MUST use commas
Pronounswho, which, thatwho, which (NEVER that)
Can you remove it?No – sentence loses meaningYes – sentence still makes sense
Defining (No Commas) The proportion of people who lived in urban areas doubled. (Without the clause, we don't know which people.)
The amount of electricity that was generated by wind power doubled.
Non-Defining (With Commas) Car sales reached 50,000 units in 2010, which was the highest figure recorded. (Without the clause, the sentence still makes sense.)
France, which produced the most nuclear power in 2000, experienced a minor drop.

6.2 Three Patterns for Non-Defining Clauses

Pattern A: Data Appending Formula: [Main Trend] , which + [Verb] + [Data/Ranking].

Coffee consumption rose to 300 tons, which accounted for over half of total beverage sales.
The unemployment rate dipped to 3% in 2018, which was the lowest point in two decades.
Pattern B: Interrupted Clause Formula: [Subject] , which + [Detail] , [Main Verb + Data].

Coal, which had been the leading fuel source in 1990, was completely replaced by natural gas by 2010.
Company A, which started the decade with just $1 million in sales, grew rapidly to reach $10 million.
Pattern C: Spatial & Level Descriptions (, where...) Formula: [Main Trend] to [Data Point] , where + [Subject + Verb].

The figure plummeted to 0.3 billion dollars, where it remained stable until the end of the period.
The population surged in City A, where housing costs increased proportionately.
⚠️ The Three Most Common Punctuation Traps 1. ❌ Car sales hit a peak in 2010 which was the highest figure.  →  ✅ Car sales hit a peak in 2010, which was the highest figure.
2. ❌ Car sales hit a peak in 2010, that was the highest figure.  →  ✅ Car sales hit a peak in 2010, which was the highest figure.
3. ❌ The figure fell to 0.3 billion, which it remained stable.  →  ✅ The figure fell to 0.3 billion, where it remained stable.

6.4 Using "That" Correctly

You can use that instead of which or who ONLY in defining relative clauses.

❌ The UK, that produced the most coal, saw a decline. (Non-defining = needs which)
✅ The UK, which produced the most coal, saw a decline.
✅ The energy that was produced by coal saw a decline. (Defining = that is fine)

7. Reduced Relative Clauses

What is a Reduced Relative Clause? Reducing relative clauses strips away the relative pronoun (which, who, that) and helping verbs (was, were, is), leaving a concise participle phrase. This is a Band 8+ technique.

7.2 Active Reduction (-ing Participle)

When the verb in the relative clause is active, delete the relative pronoun and change the verb to -ing.

Full Relative ClauseReduced Relative Clause
...sales increased to $5M in 2012, which represented a 10% growth....sales increased to $5M in 2012, representing a 10% growth.
The number of students who were attending university doubled.The number of students attending university doubled.
...the trend reversed in 2015, which brought figures down to zero....the trend reversed in 2015, bringing figures down to zero.
Car sales peaked at 50,000 in 2010, which represented a 20% increase.Car sales peaked at 50,000 in 2010, representing a 20% increase.
The population of City A surged to 2 million, which made it the most crowded region.The population of City A surged to 2 million, making it the most crowded region.

7.3 Passive Reduction (-ed / 3rd Form Participle)

When the verb in the relative clause is passive (that was generated, which were consumed), delete the relative pronoun AND the helping verb (was/were/is/are).

Full Relative ClauseReduced Relative Clause
The proportion of energy that was generated by solar power rose.The proportion of energy generated by solar power rose.
The amount of oil which was consumed by industry fell.The amount of oil consumed by industry fell.
The items that are produced on this assembly line are exported.The items produced on this assembly line are exported.
The number of vehicles which were exported to Germany fell sharply.The number of vehicles exported to Germany fell sharply.
The total budget that was allocated to healthcare remained stable.The total budget allocated to healthcare remained stable.

7.4 Four Essential Reduced Clause Patterns

TypeFormulaExample
Active (End)[Ind. Clause] + , + [Verb-ing] + [Data/Result]Female employment rose to 45%, making it the highest figure.
Passive (Middle)[Noun] + [Verb-3] + [by/in/for...] + [Main Verb]The number of vehicles exported to Germany fell sharply.
Interrupted Active[Subject] + , + [Verb-ing + Data] + , + [Main Verb]Japan, starting at just 10%, ended the decade at 80%.
Interrupted Passive[Subject] + , + [Verb-3 + Fact] + , + [Main Verb]Coal, previously considered the cheapest option, became the most expensive.
⚠️ The "Comma Splice" Trap with Reduced Clauses ❌ Sales dropped to 10% representing a new low.
✅ Sales dropped to 10%, representing a new low.

8. Multi-Clause Chains & Super Sentences

8.1 The Three Core Multi-Clause Patterns

PatternFormulaExample
Trend-Action Chain[Main Clause] + , + [Participle (-ing)] + before + [Time Clause]Organization D experienced fluctuations, increasing to 0.6 billion before falling to 0.3 billion.

8.2 The "Super Sentence" (Band 9)

Template: [Participle Opening] + [Subject] + , [Relative Clause] , + [Main Verb Phrase] + [Participle of Consequence] + before + [Time Closing]

Example 1 (Line Graph) Starting at a modest 15% in 2000, the percentage of households with broadband, which initially lagged behind dial-up, surged dramatically over the next decade, overtaking its rival in 2006 before peaking at 95% in 2020.

Anatomy:
Participle Opening: Starting at a modest 15% in 2000
Subject: the percentage of households with broadband
Embedded Relative: , which initially lagged behind dial-up,
Main Verb Phrase: surged dramatically over the next decade
Participle of Consequence: , overtaking its rival in 2006
Time Closing: before peaking at 95% in 2020.
Example 2 (Process Diagram) After being harvested by hand, the coffee beans, which are still in their red outer layer, are transported to a processing plant where they are washed, fermented, and eventually dried under the sun, a method that preserves their natural flavour.
⚠️ The "Rule of 3" Never chain more than 3 ideas (1 Main Clause + 2 Dependent Clauses) in a single sentence.
❌ While car sales dropped, truck sales rose to 50,000, which was a record high, before falling again in 2015, where they leveled off, although prices were rising. (Too many clauses = Unclear)
✅ While car sales dropped, truck sales rose to 50,000, which was a record high. However, they fell again in 2015, where they leveled off. (Two sentences = Clear and controlled)

9. Special Syntactic Structures

9.1 Grammatical Inversion

Inversion flips the standard Subject + Verb order, placing a descriptive phrase or location first. Creates emphasis and academic tone.

Standard Order: A renewable energy gold mine was buried in those decaying leaves.
Inverted Structure: Buried in those decaying leaves was a renewable energy gold mine.

Standard: A shopping centre is located at the centre of the map.
Inverted: Located at the centre of the map is a shopping centre.

9.2 Fronted Object/Reporting Clauses

Placing the data or result first prioritizes key information over its source.

Standard Order: Early research predicted that just 4kg of the dried plant would meet a family's daily needs.
Fronted Clause: Just 4kg of the dried plant would meet a family's daily energy needs, early research predicted.

9.3 Apposition

Add a clarifying noun phrase after a data point without a relative clause. Very concise and academic.

The figure reached 90% in 2020, a record high.
Tourist arrivals plummeted to 1 million, the lowest number in a decade.
Sales peaked at 50,000 units, an all-time high for the company.

9.4 The "With" Absolute Construction

Car sales increased fivefold, with the figure reaching 500,000 units in 2025.
The population grew steadily, with the highest growth occurring in urban areas.

9.5 Noun Clauses for Emphasis

What is striking is that the unemployment rate in City B halved.
What is most noticeable is the complete reversal in the fortunes of X and Y.

10. Time Markers: "In" vs "By"

10.1 The "In + Year" Structure

Rule: Use Simple Past for past years.

Formula: In + [Past Year] + , + [Subject] + [Past Tense Verb]

In 1995, coal was the primary source of electricity generation.
Car sales plummeted to a record low of 10,000 units in 2008.

Future Predictions:

Formula: In + [Future Year] + , + [Subject] + is predicted/expected to + [Verb]

In 2050, the proportion of elderly citizens is expected to rise to 35%.
Total carbon emissions are projected to fall below 10 million tons in 2030.

Placement Options:

PlacementPunctuationExample
FrontComma after time phraseIn 2005, domestic travel accounted for 60% of all flights.
EndNo commaDomestic travel accounted for 60% of all flights in 2005.

Strategic Uses:

  • Setting the starting point: In 2000, consumption stood at 40 pounds per capita.
  • Pinpointing peaks: Visitors hit a peak of 2 million in 2015.
  • Contrasting start and end: In 1990, just 10% used the internet; however, in 2000, this figure surged to 65%.

10.2 The "By + Time" Structure

Rule: Use Past Perfect (had + past participle) for past years.

Formula: By + [Past Year] + , + [Subject] + had + [Verb-3] + obj.

Incorrect: By 2010, car sales reached 50,000. (Grammar error)
Correct: By 2010, car sales had reached 50,000.

Although coal was the dominant energy source in 1980, by 2000, it had fallen to second place.
By the end of the period, the population of both cities had doubled.

Future Predictions: Use Future Perfect (will have + past participle).

Formula: By + [Future Year] + , + [Subject] + will have + [Verb-3] + obj.

By 2030, the number of elderly citizens is expected to have overtaken the youth population.
By 2050, the country will have completed its transition to renewable energy.
⚠️ The Critical Difference: "In" vs "By"
PrepositionMeaningRequired TenseIELTS Task 1 Example
In + YearIsolated event during that specific yearSimple PastIn 2015, the unemployment rate dropped to 4%.
By + YearCumulative result leading up to that yearPast PerfectBy 2015, the unemployment rate had dropped to 4%.

11. Verb Types for Task 1

Understanding these three verb types is crucial because the verb you choose dictates how the rest of your sentence must be built.

11.1 Intransitive Verbs (No Object Needed)

An intransitive verb represents an action that is complete on its own. It does not take a direct object.

Mental Test: Can you put a period right after the verb and still have a complete sentence? If yes, it's intransitive.

Common Task 1 Verbs: rise, fall, increase, decrease, fluctuate, peak, drop, grow

Formula: Subject + Verb  →  Prices rose.
Formula: Subject + Verb + Adverb  →  Car sales fluctuated wildly.
Formula: Subject + Verb + Prepositional Phrase  →  The unemployment rate dropped by 5%.

11.2 Transitive Verbs (Object Required)

A transitive verb transfers action from the subject to a receiver. It must have a direct object.

Mental Test: If you stop after the verb, does someone immediately ask "What?" If yes, it's transitive.

Common Task 1 Verbs: show, illustrate, consume, produce, manufacture, spend, represent

Formula: Subject + Verb + Direct Object  →  The pie chart illustrates the changes in energy usage.
Formula: Subject + Verb + Object  →  In 2010, the USA consumed the most fossil fuels.

Incorrect: The pie chart illustrates. (Illustrates what?)
Correct: The pie chart illustrates the changes in energy usage.

11.3 Linking Verbs (The "Equals Sign")

Linking verbs do not show action. They connect the subject to a word that describes or identifies it (called a subject complement).

Mental Test: Can you replace the verb with an equals sign (=) or the word is/was and still have the sentence make sense? If yes, it's a linking verb.

Common Task 1 Verbs: be (is, are, was, were), remain, stay, become, appear

Formula: Subject + Verb + Adjective/Noun  →  Coal was the primary energy source.
Formula: Subject + Verb + Adjective  →  The figures remained stable.
Formula: Subject + Verb + Adjective  →  Nuclear power became more popular.
Verb TypeFunctionNeed an Object?Task 1 Example
IntransitiveShows independent action❌ NoSales fell.
TransitiveAction done to something✅ YesFrance produced wine.

21. Active and Passive Voice Expanded

Based on the "Process diagram and Map.pdf"

21.1 Core Lecture – Active vs. Passive Voice

Every sentence in English has a "voice" that tells us the relationship between the Subject, the Verb, and the Object.

Active Voice (The "Who Did It" Voice): The subject performs the action. Subject + Verb + Object

The factory workers pack the boxes.

Passive Voice (The "What Happened" Voice): The object receiving the action becomes the star. Object + "To Be" verb + Past Participle (Verb 3)

The boxes are packed.
Golden Rule of Passive Voice You must have a "To Be" verb (is, are, was, were, has been) + the Past Participle (V3 – built, grown, demolished, taken).

21.2 When to Use Passive Voice

  • When the doer (agent) is unknown: The window was broken last night.
  • When focusing on the action rather than the doer: A new policy has been introduced to improve security.
  • In formal or scientific writing: The experiment was conducted successfully.

21.3 When to Use Active Voice

While passive voice is preferred for processes and maps, you can use active voice when the subject is clear and relevant, such as in introductions:

The map shows how the town has changed from 1990 to 2020.

21.4 Why Passive for Map Questions?

Maps often describe changes in landscapes, cities, or infrastructure over time. The focus is on what has changed rather than who made the changes.

Passive: A new shopping mall was built in the city center.
Active: They built a new shopping mall in the city center. (less formal)
  • Formal and Objective Tone: Passive voice sounds more academic and impersonal. The old factory was demolished, and a park was constructed.
  • When the Doer is Unknown or Irrelevant: Several roads were expanded, and a new bridge was added.
  • Better Cohesion and Clarity: Using passive helps maintain a smooth flow without repeating subjects.

21.5 Why Passive for Process Diagrams?

  • Focus on the Process, Not the Doer: The raw materials are heated at high temperatures. (vs. Workers heat the raw materials.)
  • Impersonal & Formal Tone: Passive voice is expected in academic writing.
  • Common in Scientific and Industrial Processes: The mixture is filtered to remove impurities.
Note If a process includes natural phenomena (e.g., water cycle), active voice might be acceptable: The sun heats the water, and evaporation occurs.

21.6 How to Write in Passive Structure – Step by Step

Step 1: Identify the subject, verb, and object.

Active: The chef cooked a delicious meal.
Subject: The chef  |  Verb: cooked  |  Object: a delicious meal

Step 2: Move the object to the beginning. Use the correct form of "be" + past participle.

Passive: A delicious meal was cooked (by the chef).

2.5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Copying the Question

INCORRECT "The chart below gives information about the money spent by British parents on their children's sports between 2008 and 2014."
CORRECT (paraphrased) "The line graphs show the average monthly amount that parents in Britain spent on their children's sporting activities from 2008 to 2014."

Mistake 2: No Overview

INCORRECT "In 2008, British parents spent £20. In 2009, they spent £22. In 2010, they spent £24…"
CORRECT "It is clear that parents spent more money each year on their children's participation in sports over the six-year period."

Mistake 3: Describing Items Separately

INCORRECT "Company A produced 12 tonnes. Company B produced 8 tonnes. Company C produced 4 tonnes."
CORRECT "In 2000, company A produced 12 tonnes of waste, while companies B and C produced around 8 tonnes and 4 tonnes respectively."

Mistake 4: Too Many Numbers

INCORRECT "In 1995 it was 12%, in 1996 it was 13%, in 1997 it was 15%, in 1998 it was 14%, in 1999 it was 16%…"
CORRECT "The percentage fluctuated between 12% and 16% before rising to 22% in 2000."

Mistake 5: Wrong Tense

INCORRECT "In 2000, the company produces 12 tonnes of waste."
CORRECT "In 2000, the company produced 12 tonnes of waste."

Mistake 6: No Comparisons

INCORRECT "Switzerland had sales of €3 million. The UK had sales of €15 million."
CORRECT "Switzerland had sales of €3 million, while the UK had significantly higher sales, at €15 million."

2.6 Sentence Structure Reference

10 Essential Sentence Structures for Task 1

1. Simple Statement

Subject + verb + object + data

2. Statement with Time Reference

Time + subject + verb + data
"In 2000, the company produced 12 tonnes of waste."

Statement A + while/whereas + Statement B
"While companies A and B saw waste output fall, the amount of waste produced by company C increased considerably."

Statement A. By contrast, Statement B.
"The USA has won a total of around 2,300 Olympic medals. By contrast, the Soviet Union has won just over 1,000 medals."

Subject + is + the most/least + adjective + (data)
"Swimming was the most popular sport."

"Tennis had the least popularity."

6. Statement with Trend Description

Subject + trend verb + adverb + (data)
"Parents' spending increased gradually over the following six years."

[Percentage] + of + subject + verb + data

8. Statement with Location (maps)

Subject + is + preposition + location

At + stage + subject + verb

2.7 Full Essay Analysis

Model Essay: Waste Production

Original Essay

"The line graph compares three companies in terms of their waste output between the years 2000 and 2015.

It is clear that there were significant changes in the amounts of waste produced by all three companies shown on the graph. While companies A and B saw waste output fall over the 15-year period, the amount of waste produced by company C increased considerably.

In 2000, company A produced 12 tonnes of waste, while companies B and C produced around 8 tonnes and 4 tonnes of waste material respectively. Over the following 5 years, the waste output of companies B and C rose by around 2 tonnes, but the figure for company A fell by approximately 1 tonne.

From 2005 to 2015, company A cut waste production by roughly 3 tonnes, and company B reduced its waste by around 7 tonnes. By contrast, company C saw an increase in waste production of approximately 4 tonnes over the same 10-year period. By 2015, company C's waste output had risen to 10 tonnes, while the respective amounts of waste from companies A and B had dropped to 8 tonnes and only 3 tonnes."

2.10 Additional Grammar Points

1. Relative Clauses

Structure: who / which / that + additional information

"The figure for swimming, which was 39% in 1990, increased to 48% in 2000."
"The UK, which had the smallest population, had the highest life expectancy."
"People who live in cities face higher costs."

2. Participle Clauses

Structure: Verb-ing or Verb-ed + additional information

"Having opened in 1863, the London underground is the oldest system."
"Compared to the other countries, China had the largest population."
"Ranging from 20°C to 30°C, temperatures remained stable."

3. Time Clauses

Structure: when / after / before + subject + verb

"When the data was collected, figures for 2000 were lower."
"After the first stage, the process continues."
"Before 2000, there were only 4 categories."

4. Result Clauses

Structure: so…that / such…that + result

"The change was so significant that it affected all categories."
"The increase was such that it doubled the previous figure."
