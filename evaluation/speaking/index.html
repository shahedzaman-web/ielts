Q1. Why do some people find it more difficult to make decisions than others?

Some people find decisions difficult because they overthink. They calculate every possible outcome and worry about making a mistake. Others are less bold — they’re afraid to commit and stick to a choice. So the difficulty is often psychological rather than practical.

Q2. Do you think people should make decisions based on logic or emotions?

I think logic should lead, with emotion as a secondary guide. Emotion can push us toward quick, regrettable choices, while logic helps us see the bigger picture. That said, completely ignoring emotion isn’t wise either — the best decisions usually balance both.

Q3. How has technology changed the way people make important decisions?

Technology, especially AI, has changed decision-making significantly. People now turn to AI for advice, which can be helpful but also biased — the AI only knows what it’s been trained on. So we have to be careful not to outsource our judgment entirely to technology.

Q4. Is it better to make decisions independently or seek advice from others?

It’s better to do both — seek advice but make the final decision yourself. Other people can offer perspective and experience you might lack, but the decision has to be yours because only you know your priorities and values. So: listen, then decide independently.

Q5. Should governments be allowed to make decisions that restrict individual freedom for the benefit of society?

In rare cases, yes — for example, when someone is a danger to themselves or others, the government should intervene. But in general, personal freedom should be protected, and restrictions should only happen when there’s a clear and serious risk to society. So the answer depends on the context, but the default should be freedom.

Q6. Can making a wrong decision sometimes be more valuable than making the right one? Why?

Yes, I think wrong decisions can be valuable. They teach lessons that success can’t — you learn what doesn’t work and why. A person who always makes the right choice may not develop resilience or judgment. So mistakes, provided we learn from them, can be more useful than easy successes.
