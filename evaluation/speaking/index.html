Q1. When do you usually eat bread?

I usually eat bread in the morning, especially for breakfast. When I was living alone at university, I often had bread with egg — I'd mix the egg with bread and then fry it. So I eat bread most days, mostly in the morning.

Q2. How important is bread in your culture?

Bread isn't very important in my culture. In Bangladesh, people prefer roti, which is a flatbread made from flour. We also have dishes made with rice, and dough is used to make local breads. So most people prefer those instead of Western-style bread.

Q3. Have you tried any kinds of bread from other countries?

No, I don't think so. Bread isn't very popular in my country, and most local shops don't stock foreign breads. So I haven't had the chance to try them.

Q4. Would you be interested in learning how to make bread?

I don't think so. As I said, bread isn't very common in my culture, and it's easily available in local groceries. So I don't really have a reason to learn how to make it.

Describe a person you know who is very competitive.

I'd like to talk about a friend of mine called Shagor, who I met during my university years. He's one of my closest university friends.

What I noticed about him is that he tries to be competitive in every situation. He's very hard-working and always tries to have an optimistic, competitive mindset. For example, during our second-year exams, everyone was performing poorly academically. Instead of getting frustrated, Shagor looked for solutions. He noted down all his mistakes, and in his improvement exam the following year, he worked hard to solve those issues and did much better.

He's also very passionate about sports. In our university football tournament, the opponent team was ahead by two goals. Shagor, who was a striker, worked extremely hard to score a goal, and although we didn't win the match, through his effort our team was able to perform better.

I think his competitive attitude is what drives him, and I hope it continues to help him improve in life.

Q1. What kinds of competitions do people like to enter in your country?

In my country, there are sports competitions and cultural competitions. Cricket is very popular — most schools, colleges, and universities organise at least one or two tournaments a year. There are also cultural programmes, like music and acting competitions, which take place in academic institutions, public venues, and on national television. A famous example was a TV show called Notun Kuri, which was very popular.

Q2. What do you think is the best kind of prize to win in a competition?

I think the competition itself is more important than just winning a prize. But prizes do motivate people. In my country, organisers sometimes offer money, and other times they give trophies or self-improvement books. If the prize comes from a respected figure, that's also a great experience for the winner.

Q3. Why do people like watching quiz shows and other competitions on TV?

I think competitions are really entertaining. People enjoy testing their problem-solving ability and general knowledge. When someone watches a quiz show, they think 'If I were in the competition, maybe I could answer that question,' and that intrigues them. So that's one of the reasons people find competitions so engaging.

Q4. How important is it to be very competitive at sport?

I think a competitive attitude is very important for a sportsperson. If they don't want to improve, it's really difficult because sports are very competitive. To hold their position in the team, they have to be strategic and maintain a confident, competitive attitude. If they can't, they might be dropped from the team.

Q5. Do you think sportspeople perform better when they play against competitors who are stronger than they are?

Yes, I think that's true. When the opponent team is stronger, it gives the team a boost — they know they have to do better. They have to put in more effort to cope with the other team. For example, I watched a match where our team's opening wasn't very good. But when the other, stronger team arrived — with really athletic players who were good at running — our team had to work much harder to keep up. So I'd say a stronger opponent does push you to do your best.

Q6. Why do some people think that taking part in sport is more important than winning?

I think some people value participation because the most important thing is being part of a team, whether you win or not. For example, I remember watching a local city programme where one team won the final, but the other team also enjoyed the whole environment and the game. Despite not winning, everyone participated and had a good time. So I think participating is the most important thing.
