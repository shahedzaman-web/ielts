Model answer (Band 8 style)

The line graph illustrates total expenditure by overseas visitors in the UK between 1980 and 2010, while the pie charts compare the primary reasons for these visits at the beginning and end of the period.

Overall, spending experienced a general upward trend over the thirty years, and holidays remained the most common motivation for travel, although their share declined as visiting friends or relatives became more popular.

In 1980, expenditure stood at £8,100 million. Despite a dip to £7,000 million in 1982, spending generally climbed, peaking at £12,000 million in 1996. Following this peak, figures fluctuated, dropping to £9,600 million in 2002 before recovering to £11,700 million in 2006. By 2010, spending had moderated to £10,600 million.

Regarding the reasons for visits, holidays remained the most popular choice, though their share declined from 44.1% in 1980 to 39.1% in 2010. Conversely, the proportion of visitors travelling to see friends or relatives saw a significant rise, growing from 18.7% to 28.2%. Business travel also became more common, increasing from 20.7% to 22.8%. In contrast, the “other” category saw a notable decrease, falling from 16.6% to 9.8% over the thirty-year period.

Targets for the next Task 1

  1. Check every number against the chart before you write. The holiday correction was good, but the peak year, 1980 figure, and 2010 figure are still wrong. One minute of checking prevents a band drop.
  2. Never join two full clauses with only a comma. Use a full stop, a semicolon, or a conjunction. Comma splices are a Band 5 punctuation issue.
  3. Use “account for”, not “account”. This is the single most common Task 1 preposition error.
  4. Vary “the figure”. Rotate: spending, expenditure, the amount, it, the number, the total.
  5. Check the introduction. After “illustrates”, you need a noun clause (how much…), not a full sentence.
