Model answer (Band 7+ style)

The bar chart compares the current health expenditure of several European countries as a percentage of their GDP in the years 2002, 2007, and 2012.

Overall, Switzerland consistently had the highest health expenditure relative to its GDP, while Estonia consistently had the lowest. Notably, the majority of countries experienced relatively stable expenditure over the decade, with only minor fluctuations.

Looking at the higher-spending nations, Switzerland maintained a rate of approximately 11% across all three years, followed by Belgium at around 10%. France's expenditure fluctuated, starting at 10% in 2002, rising to 11% in 2007, and then dropping to 9% in 2012. The Netherlands saw a decrease from 10% to 8% over the same period. Luxembourg and Denmark followed a similar overall trend; their figures were relatively high in 2002 and 2007 but dropped to just 1% in 2012.

Conversely, Lithuania and Poland showed stable figures in 2002 and 2007, before rising by 1% in 2012. Finally, Estonia recorded the lowest expenditure in all given years, remaining constant at 3%.

Targets for the next Task 1

  1. Data Accuracy: Carefully check the axes and bars. Ensure you are reporting the correct numbers (e.g., Luxembourg and Denmark dropping to 1%, Estonia at 3%).
  2. Articles: Ensure you use "the" before specific nouns (the proportion, the lowest figure).
  3. Prepositions: Use "accounted for", "rose by", and "in all given years".
  4. Possessives: Use apostrophes correctly (countries' health expenditure, the Netherlands').
