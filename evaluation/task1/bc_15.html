Model answer (Band 7.5+ style)

The two pie charts compare the sources of total world energy consumption and electricity generation in the previous year.

Overall, fossil fuels dominated both charts, but their roles differed markedly. Oil was the primary source of energy consumption, whereas coal was the leading source of electricity generation. Hydro and other renewables played a much smaller role in consumption but contributed more substantially to electricity.

In terms of energy consumption, oil accounted for the largest share at 34.3%, followed by coal at 25.1% and gas at 20.9%. Combustible renewables and waste contributed 10.6%, while nuclear power, hydro and other sources made up the remainder (6.5%, 2.2% and 0.4% respectively).

Turning to electricity generation, coal was clearly dominant at 39.8%, nearly double its share of consumption. Gas contributed 19.6%, while hydro (16.1%) and nuclear power (15.7%) were also significant. Oil, despite being the largest source of consumption, generated only 6.7% of electricity, and other sources accounted for 2.1%. Notably, combustible renewables and waste, which represented over a tenth of energy consumption, were not used for electricity generation at all.

In summary, coal’s share of electricity generation was disproportionately high compared with its share of consumption, while oil showed the opposite pattern. Hydro and nuclear played a larger role in electricity than in overall energy consumption.

Targets for the next Task 1

  1. Make direct comparisons: explicitly compare consumption and generation (e.g., coal’s share of generation is much higher than its share of consumption; oil is the reverse).
  2. Check plurals: sources, sectors, renewables.
  3. Prepositions with numbers: at 15.7%, accounted for 6.7%.
  4. Subject-verb agreement: renewables … were recorded.
  5. Formal vocabulary: avoid “&” in academic writing; use “and”.
