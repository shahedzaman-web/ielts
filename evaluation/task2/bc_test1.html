It is often argued that money spent on space exploration is a complete waste and that governments should spend this money on other things, for instance, health, education and poverty, to benefit the nation. I partly agree with this statement because some underdeveloped countries on different continents are struggling to provide basic healthcare and education.

In conclusion, governments should definitely focus more on ensuring basic human needs like quality education and healthcare facilities; however, they should also invest a certain amount of money in space exploration to develop satellites and space rockets to benefit the people.

Model answer (Band 7+ style)

It is sometimes argued that the enormous sums of money devoted to space exploration are wasted, and that governments should redirect this funding towards more pressing domestic priorities. While I largely agree that essential public services must come first, I believe the claim that space exploration is a “complete waste” is too strong.

On the one hand, there is a compelling case for prioritising basic human needs. In many countries, both developed and developing, significant sections of the population still lack access to quality education, affordable healthcare and adequate housing. A government that continues to fund multi-billion-dollar space programmes while its citizens struggle to pay for medicine is, in my view, misallocating public resources. Investment in schools, hospitals and poverty alleviation produces immediate and measurable benefits for the nation, whereas the returns from space exploration are speculative and often accrue over decades. For instance, the United States spends over $22 billion a year on space exploration while homelessness and medical debt remain serious problems, which suggests that spending priorities deserve careful re-examination.

On the other hand, it would be an overstatement to describe space expenditure as a complete waste. Space technology underpins satellite communications, weather forecasting, navigation systems and many medical technologies that people rely on daily. Without continued investment in satellites and space stations, these services would deteriorate. A balanced approach is therefore preferable: governments should protect core spending on health and education while maintaining a modest, carefully budgeted space programme.

In conclusion, while I agree that essential public services should be the first priority, calling space exploration a total waste is too extreme. The most sensible approach is to rebalance budgets so that urgent human needs are met without abandoning the scientific and practical benefits of space research.

Targets for the next Task 2

  2. Complete sentences: never end a sentence after an “Although / While / Because” clause — it must be joined to a main clause.
  3. Spelling and word formation: quality (not qualityful), convenient, government, prioritise, facilities.
  4. Collocation: invest in, spend money on, focus on + -ing, provide education free of charge.
  5. Split long sentences: do not join four ideas with three “and”s — use full stops and linkers instead.
