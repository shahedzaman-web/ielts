Model answer (Band 7.5+ style)

The bar chart compares the percentage of adult men who smoked in seven countries, together with the global average, in 2000 and 2015.

Overall, smoking rates declined in most of the countries shown, and the world average also fell. Armenia had the highest proportion of male smokers in both years, while Australia consistently had one of the lowest.

In 2000, Armenia topped the chart at approximately 66%, followed by Bangladesh at nearly 62% and Cuba at around 52%. The other four countries were much lower: Mexico, Egypt and Lebanon were all close to 35%, while Australia stood at about 28%. The worldwide average was 42%, meaning that Armenia, Bangladesh and Cuba all exceeded it substantially.

By 2015, the picture had changed considerably. Armenia and Cuba jointly recorded the highest figure at just over 50%, with Egypt close behind at just below 50% and Lebanon at 43%. Bangladesh had fallen to 38%, while Mexico and Australia had dropped to 20% and 17% respectively. The global average had also declined, from 42% to around 34%.

Comparing the two years, the most striking change was in Bangladesh, which fell from nearly 62% to 38%. Armenia’s figure declined by around 16 percentage points, and the world average dropped by approximately 8 points, confirming that male smoking became less common overall.

Targets for the next Task 1

  1. Check every article: the highest, the other four, the worldwide proportion.
  2. Check plurals: male smokers, proportions, rates.
  3. Check subject–verb agreement: the proportion of smokers was, the proportions were.
  4. Make at least one direct comparison: “Armenia fell from 66% to 50%”, “the world average declined by 8 percentage points”.
  5. Use precise nouns: adult men (not grown men), smoking rates (not the smoking).
