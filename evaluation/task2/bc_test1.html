YOUR ANSWER, CORRECTED

Your ideas, your structure and your sentences, with the errors fixed. Four things were changed: spelling and word form, the broken sentence in ¶3, the unsupported “5%” statistic, and one added example in ¶2. With these changes, this essay would score about Band 6.0–6.5.

People have different views about schools being very competitive and having a negative effect on children. Others believe the competitive environment encourages children to study hard and achieve academic success. In my opinion, a competitive environment can in some cases improve academic results; however, overdoing it can affect negatively.

Firstly, a competitive environment can boost a student's motivation and help them stay focused for long hours. When a student is preparing for a competitive exam, the surrounding environment helps them to focus and to study for long hours. Students can also find a study partner and discuss the current topic of study, which helps them understand that topic better and remember it for longer. Other people's focus and dedication can also help a student to motivate themselves. For instance, in countries such as South Korea and Singapore, students in highly competitive classrooms often form study groups and push each other to revise for longer hours, which visibly raises their exam performance. Therefore, a competitive environment increases focus and motivates students to study.

On the other hand, an overly competitive attitude can sometimes increase mental pressure and subsequently lead to depression and anxiety. When students study hard and sacrifice their personal and social lives but still do not get the desired results, while others achieve their desired results effortlessly, this can lead to deep frustration and anxiety. An excessively competitive environment can even lead to severe mental-health problems, and in some countries intense academic pressure has been linked to rising rates of student depression. Therefore, excessive competition should not be practised in schools, and guardians should not always compare their own children with others.

In conclusion, the competitive environment can be beneficial to some extent, as it can improve focus and motivation; however, overdoing it can affect children's mental health.

Model answer (Band 7.5+ style)

It is often argued that schools have become excessively competitive and that this harms children, while others maintain that competition motivates students to achieve. In my view, a moderate degree of competition is beneficial, but excessive pressure is damaging.

On the one hand, a competitive environment can be a powerful motivator. When students know that their classmates are working hard, they tend to work harder themselves, and the presence of high-achieving peers can raise the standard for everyone. Competition also teaches resilience and prepares young people for the realities of university admissions and the job market, where they will inevitably be compared with others. In South Korea, for example, the intense competition to enter top universities has produced one of the most highly educated workforces in the world, and many students report that the pressure helped them develop discipline they would not otherwise have acquired.

On the other hand, when competition becomes excessive, the costs outweigh the benefits. Students who sacrifice sleep, friendships and hobbies in pursuit of higher grades frequently suffer from anxiety, depression and burnout. The most damaging form of competition is comparison with peers who succeed effortlessly, because it can convince struggling students that their efforts are pointless. There is growing evidence in several East Asian countries that intense academic pressure has contributed to rising rates of adolescent mental illness, which suggests that schools need to reconsider how much emphasis they place on ranking and results.

In conclusion, while a reasonable level of competition can encourage effort and raise standards, schools should guard against excessive pressure. A balanced approach — one that rewards improvement rather than only the highest marks — is likely to produce both achievement and wellbeing.
