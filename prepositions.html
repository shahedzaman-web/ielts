What Are Prepositions?

Prepositions are small words that describe relationships with other words in a sentence, such as where something took place (in a park), when (at noon), why (because of hard work), or how something happens (with ease).

💡 Key idea: Prepositions are always followed by a noun (or pronoun) – the object of the preposition – forming a prepositional phrase.

The book on the table.
She walked through the park.
We met at noon.

Types of Prepositions

Prepositions of Place

Show where something is or where something happened.

They're building an amusement park near my apartment.
I have a sandwich in my bag.
Meet me at the restaurant.
The cat is under the table.
The health food is between the snacks and the sodas.
I'm cleaning the dust above the refrigerator.

Prepositions of Time

Show when something happened or will happen.

We have to be ready at 5:00 pm.
Don't eat before bedtime.
I get energy after the sun sets.
Your homework is due by Monday.
Our teacher fell asleep during recess.

Prepositions of Direction / Movement

Show how something is moving or which way it's going.

The dog ran across the entire field.
She took her first steps toward her mother.
He walked into a river!
It's easier to go through the woods.
He shot the ball over his opponent's head.

Prepositions of Manner, Cause, or Purpose

Show how or why something happens.

He takes me to school by car.
She drives like a maniac.
He competes for honor, not money.
Everyone was eating in silence.
Ava reads with difficulty.

Additional Types

  • Possession: of, with – The tree of our neighbor; the dog with black spots.
  • Source: from, by – The message is from outer space; made by a mouse.
  • Measurement: of, by – a cup of flour; taffy sold by the yard.

In, On, At – Prepositions of Time & Place

These three prepositions are the most common and often confusing. Here's a breakdown of their uses.

As Prepositions of Time

IN

  • • months: in August
  • • seasons: in summer
  • • years: in 1982
  • • centuries: in the 20th century
  • • times of day: in the morning
  • • periods: in a few minutes
I was born in 1982.

ON

  • • days: on Monday
  • • dates: on July 4th
  • • holidays: on Christmas Day
  • • weekend (AmE): on the weekend
I was born on July 4th.

AT

  • • clock times: at 5 pm
  • • mealtimes: at dinner
  • • specific times: at dawn, at night
  • • weekend (BrE): at the weekend
I was born at exactly 2am.

As Prepositions of Place

IN

  • • inside something: in the room
  • • countries/cities: in France
  • • the sky: in the sky
The cat is in the kennel.

ON

  • • surface: on the table
  • • streets: on Main Street
  • • floors: on the fifth floor
  • • planets: on Mars
The cat is on the table.

AT

  • • precise location: at the bus stop
  • • events: at the concert
  • • specific addresses: at 720 Main Street
We can meet at the crossroads.

⚠️ Important: Do not use in, on, at with next or last when they are used as adjectives before a time period (e.g., next Friday, last night).

Let's go on next Friday → Let's go next Friday.
We stayed home at last night → We stayed home last night.

Preposition List & Collocations

Common Prepositions (A–Z)

aboard, about, above, across, after, against, along, alongside, amid, among, around, as, at, before, behind, below, beneath, beside, between, beyond, but, by, concerning, despite, down, during, except, for, from, in, inside, into, like, near, of, off, on, onto, opposite, outside, over, past, per, plus, regarding, since, than, through, to, toward, under, underneath, unlike, until, up, upon, versus, via, with, within, without.

Common Collocations (verb + prep)

  • agree with – I agree with you.
  • believe in – Do you believe in ghosts?
  • depend on – It depends on the weather.
  • listen to – Listen to the music.
  • look at – Look at that picture.
  • wait for – I'm waiting for you.
  • apologize for – Apologize for your mistake.
  • suffer from – She suffers from allergies.
  • concentrate on – Concentrate on your work.
  • apply for – Apply for a job.

💡 Tip: Many adjectives and nouns also have specific prepositions (e.g., interested in, good at, afraid of, reason for).
