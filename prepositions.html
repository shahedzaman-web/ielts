What Are Prepositions?

Prepositions are small words that describe relationships with other words in a sentence — where (in a park), when (at noon), why (because of hard work) or how (with ease).

💡 Key idea: A preposition is always followed by its object — a noun or pronoun — forming a prepositional phrase.

The book on the table.
She walked through the park.
We met at noon.
📋

The 15 Rules — In Detail

Every rule includes the core principle, extra explanation, real examples, and the traps examiners love to test.

1 Prepositions of Place

Show where something is or where something happened. The most common are in (enclosed), on (surface), at (point).

IN — enclosed

The keys are in my pocket.
She lives in Paris.

ON — surface

The book is on the shelf.
The cat is on the roof.

AT — point

He's at the bus stop.
We met at the cinema.

underbelow somethingThe cat is under the table.
overabove, coveringThere's a bridge over the river.
above / belowhigher / lower (not touching)The plane flew above the clouds.
between / amongtwo / more than twoShe sat between John and Mary.
behindat the back ofThe car is behind the house.
beside / next toat the side ofShe sat beside me.
nearclose toThey live near the park.
oppositefacingThe bank is opposite the school.

Show how something is moving or which way it is going.

4 Prepositions of Manner

Show how something happens. The key ones are by (means), with (using something), in (in a certain way), like (similarity), as (role).

She smiled with joy.
He spoke in a whisper.
She writes with a pen.
She sings like a bird.
He works as a teacher.
They celebrated in style.
⚠️ Watch out: like = similar to; as = in the role of. “He works as a teacher” (his job) vs “He works like a teacher” (in the same way as a teacher).

5 Agent & Instrument — by vs with

By is used for the doer of an action (agent). With is used for the tool or object used to do it (instrument).

The letter was written by Shakespeare. (agent)
The window was broken by a ball. (cause)
She cut the bread with a knife. (instrument)
He painted it with a brush.
⚠️ Watch out: by is also used with means of transport (by car) and with -ing forms (by working hard).

6 Purpose & Reason — for, to, because of, due to

Show why something happens. The main options are for + noun, to + verb, and because of / due to / owing to + noun phrase.

I'm studying for an exam. (noun)
I'm studying to pass the exam. (verb)
Thank you for your help.
He was punished for cheating.
The game was cancelled because of rain.
The flight was delayed due to fog.
💡 Rule of thumb: for + noun / -ing, to + base verb, because of / due to + noun phrase.

7 Possession & Measure — of, with, to

Of is the main preposition of possession and measure. With describes a feature. To is used with the key to a door and the answer to a question.

The roof of the house.
A cup of tea.
A piece of cake.
A group of students.
A man with a beard.
The key to the door.
The answer to the question.

8 Source & Origin — from, out of, off

Show where something comes from or where it started.

She comes from Spain.
This wine is from France.
He ran out of the room.
She took the book off the shelf.
The letter is from my aunt.
I borrowed money from him.
⚠️ Watch out: from = origin, out of = movement from inside to outside, off = away from a surface.

9 In / On / At — Time (the full picture)

IN

months · seasons · years · centuries · parts of day · periods

in August · in summer · in 1982 · in the morning

ON

days · dates · holidays · the weekend (AmE)

on Monday · on July 4th · on Christmas Day

AT

clock times · mealtimes · precise moments · the weekend (BrE)

at 5 pm · at dinner · at dawn · at night
⚠️ Watch out: in the morning but at night. Also at noon / at midnight take no article after at.

10 In / On / At — Place (the full picture)

IN

inside something · countries · cities · the sky

in the room · in France · in the sky

ON

surfaces · streets · floors · planets

on the table · on Main Street · on the fifth floor

AT

precise locations · events · addresses

at the bus stop · at the concert · at 720 Main Street
⚠️ Watch out: we say on the bus but in a car; at the cinema (event) but in the cinema (building).

11 Means of Transport — by, in, on

Most forms of transport take by with no article. But in and on are used when there is an article or possessive.

PatternExample
by + transport (no article)by car · by bus · by train · by plane · by bike · by boat
on + transport (with article)on the bus · on the train · on the plane · on the 8 o'clock train
in + transport (with article)in a taxi · in a car · in a helicopter
on footI go to school on foot.
into / out of / off + vehicleget into the car · get out of the car · get off the bus
💡 Test: if you can say “by + noun” with no article, use by. Otherwise use in or on.

12 Time Expressions — No Preposition Needed

We do not use in / on / at before next, last, this, every, tomorrow, yesterday, today.

✅ Let's go next Friday. (not on next Friday)
✅ We stayed home last night. (not at last night)
✅ I'm busy this afternoon.
✅ She comes every day.
✅ I saw him yesterday.
✅ We'll talk tomorrow.
⚠️ Exception: in the morning / afternoon / evening does take the; at night / noon / midnight does not.

📌
How to choose a preposition: (1) Is it place, time, movement or manner? (2) Is it a fixed collocation with a verb, adjective or noun? (3) Is it a time expression like next / last / this that needs no preposition? Answer those three questions and the right preposition will usually appear.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between in, on and at?

Think of them as three levels of precision. In = enclosed or large area (in the room, in France). On = a surface (on the table, on the wall). At = a point (at the door, at the bus stop). The same logic applies to time: in = long period, on = day/date, at = precise clock time.

Why do we say “in the morning” but “at night”?

This is an idiom — the pattern goes back to Old English and doesn't follow a logical rule. Parts of the day take in the (in the morning / afternoon / evening), but night, noon and midnight take at (at night, at noon, at midnight).

When do I use “by” and when “with”?

By = the doer of an action (written by Shakespeare) or a method (by car, by working hard). With = the tool or object used (cut with a knife, written with a pen).

Why is “on next Friday” wrong?

Because next, last, this, every, yesterday, tomorrow already contain the prepositional meaning of time. Adding on is redundant. Say “next Friday”, not “on next Friday”.

How can I remember preposition collocations?

Learn them as whole chunks — “depend on”, “afraid of”, “solution to” — rather than learning the preposition separately. Use flashcards with the full chunk on one side and the meaning on the other. The 150 exercises on this page are a good place to start.
