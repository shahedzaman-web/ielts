Why Articles & Countability Matter

Articles — a, an, the — are the most common words in English, and also the most frequently wrong. Whether you need an article at all depends on whether the noun is countable or uncountable, and whether it is general or specific.

💡 The golden triangle: a/an for one non-specific countable noun · the for a specific one · ∅ (no article) for general plural or uncountable nouns.

📋

The 17 Rules — In Detail

Every rule includes the core principle, extra explanation, real examples, and the traps examiners love to test.

1 Countable & Uncountable 1

A noun can be countable or uncountable. This affects which articles and quantifiers you can use.

✅ Countable

Can be singular or plural (banana / bananas), can take numbers, use a/an with singular and some/any with plural.

I eat a banana every day.
Kate was singing a song.

❌ Uncountable

Only one form (rice, no plural), cannot take numbers, no a/an, use some/any.

I eat rice every day.
Kate was listening to music.
CountableUncountable
Examplesa dog, two dogs, some dogswater, music, rice, money
Use a/an✅ a banana❌ a rice
Use alone❌ I like banana.✅ I like rice.
Use some/any✅ some apples✅ some water
Use many/few✅ many pictures❌ many water
Use much/little❌ much apples✅ much water
⚠️ Watch out: uncountable nouns are still singular in grammar — “The news is good”, “Money makes the world go round”.

2 Countable & Uncountable 2 — Meaning Change

Many nouns can be both countable and uncountable, with a difference in meaning.

NounCountable meaningUncountable meaning
noisea noise (a specific sound) — Did you hear a noise?noise in general — There's too much noise.
papera paper (a newspaper / document) — I bought a paper.material — I need some paper.
haira hair (one strand) — There's a hair in my soup!all hair on a head — You've got long hair.
rooma room (a space in a building) — a nice roomspace — There isn't room.
experiencean experience (an event) — interesting experiencesknowledge — a lot of experience
glassa glass (a drinking vessel) — a glass of watermaterial — made of glass
chickena chicken (the animal) — two chickensthe meat — I eat chicken.
businessa business (a company) — He owns a business.activity — I'm here on business.
lighta light (a lamp) — Turn on the light.illumination — Light travels fast.

Drinks (coffee, tea, juice, beer) are normally uncountable, but you can say a coffee (= a cup of coffee), two coffees etc.

Two coffees and an orange juice, please.
⚠️ Watch out: “I had a lot of experience” = knowledge. “I had some interesting experiences” = events. The meaning flips with the article.

3 Countable Nouns with a/an and some

Use a/an with a singular countable noun to say what kind of thing or person. Use some with a plural countable noun (or an uncountable one).

A / An

She never wears a hat.
What a beautiful day!
My father is a doctor.
Jack has a long nose.

Some

I've seen some good movies recently.
Some friends of mine are coming.
I need some new sunglasses.
Some children learn very quickly.
⚠️ Watch out: a singular countable noun cannot stand alone — She wears hat. is wrong. Say “She wears a hat.”
💡 Do not use “some” when talking about things in general: “I love bananas.” (not some bananas)

4 A / An — The Sound Rule

Choose a or an by the sound of the next word, not its first letter.

Use a before a consonant soundUse an before a vowel sound
a university (/juː/), a unicorn, a usual event, a European, a one-way street, a hotel, a useful tool an hour, an honest man, an heir, an MBA, an umbrella, an unusual event, an old man, an FBI agent
She's a university student.
He waited for an hour.
It's a one-way street.
She's an honest person.
⚠️ Watch out: “hotel” takes a because the /h/ is pronounced; “hour” takes an because the /h/ is silent.

5 a/an vs the — First Mention vs Specific

A / An

First time we mention something, or not specific.

I had a sandwich and an apple.
Tim sat down on a chair.
We stayed at a very cheap hotel.

The

Specific, already known, or unique.

The sandwich wasn't very good.
Tim sat down on the chair nearest the door.
The hotel where we stayed was cheap.

Once a week / three times a day / £1.50 a kilo — use a (not the).

I go to the cinema about once a month.
Helen works eight hours a day.

6 the 1 — Unique, Superlative & Ordinal

Unique things

The equator, the world, the sun, the moon, the earth
Our apartment is on the tenth floor.
Buenos Aires is the capital of Argentina.

Superlatives & ordinals

What's the longest river in Europe?
It's the best way to learn.
Your sweater is the same colour as mine.
She was the first to arrive.
⚠️ Watch out: “the same” always takes the — “They are the same.” Never “same” alone as a subject complement.

7 the 2 — Institutions (school / the school)

General idea — no the

Ellie goes to school.
He was taken to hospital.
Ken's brother is in prison.
I'm going to bed now.
Chris didn't go to work yesterday.
Let's go home.

Specific place — with the

Ellie's mother went to the school.
Jane went to the hospital to visit Joe.
Ken went to the prison to visit his brother.
I sat down on the bed.
I work at the office.
Workmen went to the church to repair the roof.
💡 Test: if the person is using the building for its normal purpose, use no article; if they are just visiting the building, use the.

8 the 3 — General vs Specific

In general — no the

I'm afraid of dogs.
Children learn from playing.
I couldn't live without music.
Sugar isn't very good for you.
English people drink a lot of tea.

Specific — with the

We took the children to the zoo.
I liked the music in the film.
Can you pass the sugar, please?
The English people I know drink a lot.
I like the people I work with.
💡 Compare: I like working with people. (general) vs I like the people I work with. (specific)

9 the 4 — Types, Inventions & The + Adjective

Types of things

The giraffe is the tallest of all animals.
The bicycle is an excellent means of transport.
When was the camera invented?
Can you play the guitar?

Groups of people

The rich should pay higher taxes.
We need to help the homeless.
The young have the future in their hands.
The French are famous for their food.
⚠️ Watch out: the old = old people, the French = French people — always plural, take a plural verb.
💡 Note: man (without the) = human beings in general — “What do you know about the origins of man?”

10 Geographical Names

Without the

Continents, countries, states, cities, single mountains, lakes, most islands.

Africa, South America, France, Japan, Texas, Cairo, Everest, Lake Superior

With the

Oceans, seas, rivers, canals, deserts, mountain ranges, groups of islands, countries with plural names.

the Atlantic, the Red Sea, the Nile, the Suez Canal, the Sahara, the Andes, the Canaries, the Netherlands, the Philippines, the United States, the UK
⚠️ Watch out: the north of Brazil but northern Brazil (no the before the adjective form).

11 Buildings, Places & Company Names

Without the

Streets, parks, stations, airports, universities, palaces, churches, companies.

Union Street, Hyde Park, Manchester Airport, Victoria Station, Cambridge University, Buckingham Palace, St John's Church
Fiat, Sony, Kodak, Singapore Airlines, McDonald's

With the

Hotels, theatres, museums, famous buildings, newspapers, organisations, names with of.

the Sheraton Hotel, the Palace Theatre, the Guggenheim Museum, the White House, the Washington Post, the BBC
the Bank of England, the Tower of London
💡 Shortcut: if the name contains of, use the (the Bank of England). If it contains a common noun like Hotel / Theatre / Museum / Building, use the.

12 Singular & Plural Nouns

Plural-only words (two parts)

glasses, trousers, jeans, shorts, pyjamas, binoculars, scissors

Take a plural verb: My trousers are too long.

Use a pair of: a pair of jeans

-ics nouns (singular)

athletics, gymnastics, economics, physics, electronics, maths

Gymnastics is my favourite sport.

News is singular: The news was unexpected.

Group nouns (plural verb common)

audience, committee, company, family, government, staff, team
The government have decided to increase taxes.
The police are investigating.

Sum of money, time, distance

Fifty thousand pounds was stolen.
Three years is a long time.
Ten kilometres is too far to walk.
⚠️ Watch out: people is the plural of person — “He's a nice person” but “They are nice people” (not persons in ordinary speech).

13 Noun + Noun

When two nouns sit together, the first noun modifies the second. It usually stays singular.

a bus driver = the driver of a bus
income tax = tax on income
the city centre = the centre of the city
an apple tree = a tree that has apples
a Paris hotel = a hotel in Paris
a frying pan = a pan for frying
a washing machine
a swimming pool
a coffee cup vs a cup of coffee
a shopping bag vs a bag of shopping

Compound nouns with numbers: the first noun is singular.

a three-hour journey (not three-hours)
a ten-pound note
a four-week course
two 14-year-old girls

14 Possessive -'s and of …

-'s (apostrophe + s)

Mostly for people, animals and time.

Tom's computer isn't working.
What is your sister's name?
Don't step on the cat's tail.
My sisters' room (plural owner)
the men's changing room
yesterday's newspaper
a week's holiday / three weeks' holiday

of …

For things, ideas and long phrases.

the temperature of the water
the name of the book
the owner of the restaurant
the beginning of the month
the top of the hill
the government's decision / the decision of the government
the city's streets / the world's population
⚠️ Watch out: for things, prefer of — “the name of the book” is safer than “the book's name” in formal writing.

15 Zero Article — Generalizations

When you talk about things in general — all of a class, or an abstract idea — use no article at all.

I like dogs. (not the dogs in general)
Children learn from playing.
Life is beautiful.
Water is important.
Honesty is the best policy.
Coffee keeps me awake.
Music makes me happy.
Mathematics is my favourite subject.
We study history at school.
Dogs are animals.
⚠️ Watch out: don't confuse this with specific uses — “Dogs are noisy” (all dogs) vs “The dogs next door are noisy” (specific dogs).

Frequently Asked Questions

When do I use “a” vs “an”?

By the sound, not the letter. Use a before a consonant sound (a university, a one-way street) and an before a vowel sound (an hour, an honest man).

What is the difference between countable and uncountable nouns?

Countable nouns can be counted and have a plural (a chair / two chairs). Uncountable nouns cannot be counted and have no plural (furniture, water, information). Uncountable nouns take singular verbs and no a/an.

Why do we say “go to school” but “go to the school”?

With no article, the person is using the building for its normal purpose (studying). With the, they are visiting the building specifically (e.g., a parent going to meet a teacher).

When is “the” not used before a place name?

Most countries, cities, continents, single mountains, lakes and streets take no article (France, Cairo, Everest, Lake Superior, Union Street). Oceans, rivers, deserts, mountain ranges and plural country names take the (the Nile, the Sahara, the Andes, the Netherlands).

What is the trickiest part of article usage?

Knowing whether the noun is being used generally or specifically. “I like music” (general) vs “I liked the music in the film” (specific). Always ask: am I talking about all of it, or a particular instance?
