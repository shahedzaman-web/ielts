Countable & Uncountable Nouns 1

A noun can be countable or uncountable. This affects how we use articles and quantities.

✅ Countable

  • • Can be singular or plural (banana / bananas)
  • • Can use numbers (one banana, two bananas)
  • • Use a/an with singular (a banana)
  • • Use some / any with plural (some bananas)
I eat a banana every day.
Kate was singing a song.

❌ Uncountable

  • • Only one form (rice, no plural)
  • • Cannot use numbers (not “one rice”)
  • • Cannot use a/an (not “a rice”)
  • • Use some / any (some rice)
I eat rice every day.
Kate was listening to music.

💡 Key rule: Use a/an with singular countable nouns. Use some with plural countable and uncountable nouns.

Countable & Uncountable 2

Many nouns can be both countable and uncountable, with a difference in meaning.

Countable

Did you hear a noise just now? (a specific noise)
I bought a paper. (a newspaper)
There's a hair in my soup! (one single hair)
This is a nice room. (a room in a house)
I had some interesting experiences. (things that happened)

Uncountable

There's too much noise. (noise in general)
I need some paper to write on. (material)
You've got very long hair. (all hair on your head)
There isn't room. (space)
I had a lot of experience. (knowledge from doing)

Drinks (coffee, tea, juice, beer) are normally uncountable, but you can say a coffee (= a cup of coffee), two coffees etc.

Two coffees and an orange juice, please.

Usually uncountable:

accommodation behaviour bread chaos furniture information luggage news progress scenery traffic weather work

We do not use a/an with these. I hope you have good weather. (not a good weather)

Countable vs uncountable pairs:

a job vs work
a view vs scenery
a day vs weather
a bag vs baggage/luggage
a chair vs furniture
a suggestion vs advice
a car vs traffic

Unit 70 Countable and uncountable 2

📌

Countable Nouns with a/an and some

A / An

Use a/an with singular countable nouns to say what kind of thing or person.

She never wears a hat.
What a beautiful day!
My father is a doctor.
Jack has a long nose.

⚠️ Singular countable nouns cannot be used alone: She wears hat.

Some

Use some with plural countable nouns.

I've seen some good movies recently.
Some friends of mine are coming.
I need some new sunglasses.

Some also means “some but not all”:

Some children learn very quickly.

💡 Do not use some when talking about things in general: I love bananas. (not some bananas)

Singular vs plural:

A dog is an animal. / Dogs are animals.
I'm an optimist. / We're optimists.
What a lovely dress! / What awful shoes!

Unit 71 Countable nouns with a/an and some

📖

a/an and the

A / An

First time we mention something, or not specific.

I had a sandwich and an apple.
Tim sat down on a chair.
We stayed at a very cheap hotel.

The

Specific, already known, or unique.

The sandwich wasn't very good.
Tim sat down on the chair nearest the door.
The hotel where we stayed was cheap.

Specific uses of the: light / floor / ceiling / door / carpet in a room, the bank, the post office, the doctor, the dentist.

Can you turn off the light, please?
I have to go to the bank today.
Clare isn't well. She's gone to the doctor.

Once a week / three times a day / £1.50 a kilo — use a (not the).

I go to the cinema about once a month.
Helen works eight hours a day.

Unit 73 the 1

🏫

the 2 – school / the school etc.

General idea (no the)

Ellie goes to school.
When I leave school, I plan to go to university.
He was taken to hospital.
Ken's brother is in prison.
Sarah's father goes to church.
I'm going to bed now.
Chris didn't go to work yesterday.
Let's go home.

Specific place (with the)

Ellie's mother went to the school.
I went to the university to meet a professor.
Jane has gone to the hospital to visit Joe.
Ken went to the prison to visit his brother.
Workmen went to the church to repair the roof.
I sat down on the bed.
I work at the office.

Unit 74 the 2 (school / the school etc.)

👥

the 3 – children / the children

In general (no the)

I'm afraid of dogs.
Children learn from playing.
I couldn't live without music.
Sugar isn't very good for you.
English people drink a lot of tea.
Most shops accept credit cards.

Specific (with the)

We took the children to the zoo.
I liked the music in the film.
Can you pass the sugar, please?
The English people I know drink a lot.
I like the people I work with.
The coffee we had wasn't good.

💡 Compare: I like working with people. (general) vs I like the people I work with. (specific)

Unit 75 the 3 (children / the children)

🦒

the 4 – the giraffe / the telephone / the old

Types of things

The giraffe is the tallest of all animals.
The bicycle is an excellent means of transport.
When was the camera invented?
Can you play the guitar?

Groups of people

The rich should pay higher taxes.
We need to help the homeless.
The young have the future in their hands.
The French are famous for their food.

💡 the old = old people, the French = French people (plural)

Man (without the) = human beings in general: What do you know about the origins of man?

Unit 78 Names with and without the 2

🔀

Singular & Plural

Plural words (two parts)

glasses, trousers, jeans, shorts
pyjamas, binoculars, scissors

Take a plural verb: My trousers are too long.

Use a pair of: a pair of jeans

Nouns ending in -ics

athletics, gymnastics, economics
physics, electronics, maths

Usually singular: Gymnastics is my favourite sport.

News is singular: The news was unexpected.

Group nouns (plural verb common)

audience, committee, company, family, government, staff, team
The government have decided to increase taxes.
The police are investigating.

Singular verb also possible: The government has decided…

People

He's a nice person.
They are nice people. (not persons)

Sum of money, time, distance = singular:

Fifty thousand pounds was stolen.
Three years is a long time.

Unit 79 Singular and plural

🔗

Noun + Noun (a bus driver / a headache)

a bus driver = the driver of a bus
income tax = tax on income
the city centre = the centre of the city
an apple tree = a tree that has apples
a Paris hotel = a hotel in Paris
my life story = the story of my life
a frying pan = a pan for frying
a washing machine
a swimming pool
a coffee cup vs a cup of coffee
a shopping bag vs a bag of shopping

Compound nouns with numbers: first noun is singular.

a three-hour journey (not three-hours)
a ten-pound note
a four-week course
two 14-year-old girls

Unit 80 Noun + noun (a bus driver / a headache)

✏️

-’s (your sister’s name) and of … (the name of the book)

-’s (apostrophe + s)

Mostly for people and animals.

Tom's computer isn't working.
What is your sister's name?
Don't step on the cat's tail.

Plural nouns: my sisters' room

Irregular plurals: the men's changing room

Time words: yesterday's newspaper, next week's meeting

Periods of time: a week's holiday, three weeks' holiday

of …

For things, ideas and long phrases.

the temperature of the water
the name of the book
the owner of the restaurant
the beginning of the month
the top of the hill

For organisations, both are possible:

the government's decision or the decision of the government

For places: the city's streets, the world's population
