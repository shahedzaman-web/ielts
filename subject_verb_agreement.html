What Is Subject–Verb Agreement?

Subject–verb agreement (also called concord) is the grammatical rule that the verb in a sentence must match its subject in number (singular or plural) and person (first, second or third).

💡 Core Rule: A singular subject takes a singular verb. A plural subject takes a plural verb.

✅ Singular

The boy runs fast.

Singular subject + singular verb

✅ Plural

The boys run fast.

Plural subject + plural verb

Why it matters: agreement errors are the single most common grammar mistake in academic and professional writing. They don't usually change the meaning, but they instantly signal carelessness to a reader.
📋

The 17 Rules — In Detail

Every rule below includes the rule itself, extra explanation, real examples, and the traps that examiners love to test.

1 Singular ↔ Plural (The Base Rule)

A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb. In the present simple, the only change is the -s / -es added to the third-person singular.

Regular verbs: work → he works · watch → she watches · study → it studies · go → he goes

Rachel spends her free time listening to music.
Blaine and Kurt play the piano.
She likes dessert after every meal.
The children run in the garden.
⚠️ Watch out: the -s goes on the verb, not the noun. “The dogs bark” — not “the dogs barks”.

2 The Verb Be (am / is / are / was / were)

Be is the only English verb that changes for both number and person. You must memorise this table.

PersonPronounPresentPastExample
1st singularIamwasI am confident.
2ndYouarewereYou are confident.
3rd singularHe / She / ItiswasShe is a singer.
1st pluralWearewereWe are confident.
3rd pluralTheyarewereThey are confident.
The girls are waiting for you.
I was reading the latest book.
The results were announced yesterday.
⚠️ Watch out: “You” is always plural in form, even when it means one person: “You are late,” never “you is”.
💡 Note: “Every boy and every girl was given a bar of chocolate.” — every keeps the subject singular even with and.

3 Have vs. Has

Has → third-person singular (he, she, it, a singular name). Have → I, you, we, they and every plural subject.

I have a younger brother.
Swetha has a pet dog.
Finn has been waiting to talk to you.
The students have finished their exams.
⚠️ Watch out: in “has/have + past participle” the same rule applies — “She has left”, “They have left”.

4 Compound Subjects with And

Two or more subjects joined by and normally make a plural subject → plural verb.

Krish and Radha are on their way to the airport.
Caren, Sheela and Akash have completed their assessments.
⚠️ Exception: if the two nouns describe one single idea or thing, use a singular verb — “Bread and butter is my favourite breakfast.” · “Rice and curry is a classic dish.”

5 Or / Nor — The Proximity Rule

With or or nor, the verb agrees with the subject closest to it (the nearest noun).

Celery or spring onion works fine.
The teacher or the students are coming.
Neither Ricky nor Gina is here yet.
💡 Memory trick: “the verb hugs its nearest neighbour”.

6 Indefinite Pronouns

These are always singular:

anybody · anyone · anything · everybody · everyone · everything · nobody · no one · nothing · somebody · someone · something · each · either · neither · one

Everybody loves using correct grammar!
Nobody has understood anything.
Everyone was happy with the outcome.
⚠️ Watch out: “Everybody has their own opinion” is accepted in modern usage, but the verb stays singular — only the possessive pronoun shifts.

7 Either…or / Neither…nor

The verb agrees with the noun or pronoun that comes just before the verb.

Neither the children nor their parents are aware.
Either the teacher or the students have to take initiative.
Neither the manager nor his assistants were informed.
⚠️ Watch out: when either / neither stands alone as a subject (not in a pair), it is singular: “Neither of the answers is correct.”

8 Tricky Nouns

Some nouns look plural but are singular; others are always plural.

Singular in meaningPlural in meaning
news · mathematics · physics · economics · measles · mumps · politics police · cattle · scissors · trousers · glasses · clothes · belongings
The news has been depressing.
Mathematics is my favorite subject.
The police have been looking for him.
The scissors do not work.
💡 Pair nouns: for items made of two joined parts (glasses, scissors, trousers) use a pair of + singular verb: “A pair of scissors is on the table.”

9 Negative Sentences (do / does)

Use do not with plural subjects and with I, you, we, they. Use does not with third-person singular subjects.

The children do not like trigonometry.
My father does not work at the bank anymore.
It does not matter.
⚠️ Watch out: after does, the main verb loses its -s: “She does not work”, never “does not works”.

10 Interrogative Sentences

The helping verb at the beginning must agree with the subject that follows it.

Do you read thriller novels?
Does she know you already?
Has Sharon submitted her project?
⚠️ Watch out: in wh-questions the subject may come after the verb — “Where are my keys?” (subject = keys, plural).

11 Sentences Beginning with Here / There

Here and there are never the real subject. The verb agrees with the noun that comes after it.

Here is your book.
There are new maps in the DLC.
There have been many changes.
💡 Test: flip the sentence — “Your book is here.” The subject becomes obvious.

12 Abstract & Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable / abstract nouns are treated as singular and take a singular verb: honesty, love, water, information, furniture, advice, luggage, knowledge, happiness, patience, milk.

Honesty is the best policy.
Love makes people do crazy things.
The information was very useful.
⚠️ Watch out: never add -s to uncountable nouns — “informations”, “furnitures” and “advices” do not exist.

13 Time, Distance & Money

When a quantity is treated as one single unit, use a singular verb.

1,267 kilometres is too long for us.
10 years is not considered optimum.
$1,000 is a little too much.
⚠️ Watch out: if you mean separate items, use a plural verb — “Ten dollars were lying on the table.”

14 Collective Nouns

Words like team, family, committee, jury, government, staff, audience, class can be singular (acting as one unit) or plural (members acting separately).

The team goes to practice at 4:00. (together)
The team go to their own homes. (separately)
My family is settled in Australia.
💡 Test: if you can replace the noun with it, use singular; if you can replace it with they, use plural.

15 All / Some / Most / A lot of / Half of

The verb agrees with the noun that follows the preposition of — not with the quantifier.

All of my dresses have become tight. (dresses = plural)
A lot of food is left out. (food = uncountable)
Some of the books are torn.
Half of the work is done.

16 Each / Every

When used as the subject, each and every are always singular, even when followed by of + plural noun.

Each student has been asked to provide a letter.
Every teacher, parent and student is expected to work together.
Each of the rooms was cleaned.

17 Wishes, Hypotheticals & Requests (Subjunctive)

After wish and in unreal if-clauses, use were for every subject — including I, he, she, it. After verbs of demand, suggestion and request, use the base form of the verb.

I wish I were a bird.
If you were here, I would not be sad.
We request that everyone make their choices now.
It is essential that she be on time.
💡 Triggers: suggest, recommend, demand, insist, request, propose, it is essential/vital/important that…
📌
Summary: the verb must always match the subject in number. Strip away prepositional phrases, ignore here/there, and watch out for collective nouns, indefinite pronouns and quantifiers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is subject-verb agreement?

It is the grammatical rule that the subject and verb in a sentence must share the same number and person. Apart from the verb be, English agreement is almost entirely about number.

How does subject-verb agreement work?

If the subject is singular, use the singular form of the verb; if plural, use the plural form. The verb be is the exception — it changes for both number and person (am / is / are, was / were).

What are examples of subject-verb agreement?

Cats make great pets. → plural subject + plural verb.
A cat makes a great pet. → singular subject + singular verb.

Why is subject-verb agreement important?

It keeps writing clear and correct. Agreement errors confuse readers and make even good ideas look unpolished — especially in exams, applications and professional emails.

What is the trickiest part?

Words between the subject and verb. In “The box of chocolates is on the table”, the subject is box, not chocolates. Always find the head noun before choosing the verb.
