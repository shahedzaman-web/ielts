Why Parts of Speech Decide Your IELTS Score

Every gap-fill in Listening, every summary completion in Reading, every sentence-ending match — they all ask the same hidden question: what kind of word fits here? If you can answer that, you can answer the question even when you don't understand every word.

💡 The three-skill loop: 1. Read the gap and identify the word class needed. 2. Predict the meaning area (a number? a place? a job?). 3. Listen or scan for a paraphrase of that meaning.

Part of speechWhat it doesIELTS example
Nounnames a person, place, thing or ideaThe accommodation is £120 per week.
Verbshows an action or stateThe museum opens at 9 a.m.
Adjectivedescribes a nouna heated swimming pool
Adverbdescribes a verb, adjective or another adverbThe train runs frequently.
Pronounreplaces a noun (cohesion)The researchers published their findings.

The 12 Rules — In Detail

The nine parts of speech, plus word families, prefixes/suffixes, and the IELTS answer-type prediction rule that ties everything together.

1 Nouns — The Answer Words

Nouns name a person, place, thing, or idea. In IELTS gap-fills, most answers are nouns — especially names, places, dates, prices and objects.

Types

Common: student, book
Proper: London, Dr Patel
Abstract: freedom, success
Collective: team, staff
Compound: swimming pool, check-in

IELTS signal positions

after a / an / the
after of (a lot of ___)
after an adjective (a new ___)
after a preposition (in ___, per ___)

The tour costs £15 per person. (noun after “per”)
Accommodation is available on campus. (noun after preposition)
⚠️ Watch out: in Listening, a noun answer must be spelled correctly and must not exceed the word limit (“NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS”).

2 Verbs — Actions and States

Verbs show what the subject does or what state it is in. They carry tense, so they tell you when something happens — vital for Reading timelines and Listening instructions.

TypeFunctionExample
Main verbcarries the meaningThe library closes at 8.
Auxiliaryhelps form tense / question / negativeShe has finished. Do you agree?
Modaladds possibility, obligation, adviceYou must register. It might rain.
Transitiveneeds an objectShe wrote a report.
Intransitiveno object neededThe train arrived.
Stativestate, not action (no continuous)I know the answer.
💡 IELTS use: in Reading, verbs tell you the direction of change — increase, decline, remain stable. In Listening, verbs of instruction (turn left, collect, register) are often the answer itself.

3 Adjectives — Describing Nouns

Adjectives describe or limit a noun. In IELTS they are the most common distractors — a wrong option often has the right meaning but the wrong word class.

a heated swimming pool (adjective + noun)
The experimental results were inconclusive.
⚠️ Watch out: never put an adjective where a noun is needed. “The beautiful of the city” ✗ → “The beauty of the city” ✓.
Order ruleExample
Opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → purposea lovely small old round brown French wooden table
💡 IELTS use: in Matching Headings and True/False/Not Given, adjectives like significant, marginal, dramatic, gradual carry the judgement — they decide the answer.

4 Adverbs — Describing Verbs & Adjectives

Adverbs describe how, when, where, or how much. Most are formed with -ly, but many common ones are not (fast, well, very, often, always, never).

TypeQuestion answeredExamples
MannerHow?quickly, carefully, fluently
FrequencyHow often?always, usually, rarely, never
DegreeHow much?very, extremely, slightly, highly
TimeWhen?yesterday, soon, already, recently
PlaceWhere?here, there, nearby, abroad
The train runs frequently. (adverb of frequency)
The temperature rose gradually. (adverb of manner)
⚠️ Watch out: hard vs hardly — “She works hard” (a lot) vs “She hardly works” (almost not). This is a classic IELTS Listening distractor.

5 Pronouns — The Glue of Reading

Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition. In IELTS Reading, tracking what a pronoun refers to is often the key to a Matching Information or Summary Completion answer.

TypePronounsExample
SubjectI, you, he, she, it, we, theyThey found the cause.
Objectme, you, him, her, it, us, themThe team thanked them.
Possessivemine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirsThe idea was theirs.
Reflexivemyself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselvesThey built it themselves.
Relativewho, whom, which, that, whoseThe study, which was published in 2020, …
Demonstrativethis, that, these, thoseThese findings suggest …
The researchers published their findings. (= the researchers' findings)
💡 IELTS use: when a paragraph starts with “This suggests…”, the answer is almost always in the previous sentence. Follow the pronoun backwards.

6 Prepositions — Small Words, Big Traps

Prepositions show the relationship of a noun to the rest of the sentence: time, place, direction, cause. In Listening they decide whether an answer is at 5 p.m. or by 5 p.m.

UsePrepositionExample
Month / year / seasoninin July, in 2019, in summer
Day / dateonon Monday, on 4 July
Clock timeatat 9 a.m., at noon
Deadlinebysubmit by Friday
Durationforfor two hours
Starting pointsincesince 2010
Price per unitper£15 per person
Increase / decreaseina rise in demand
Reasonforthe reason for the decline
Solution / answer / keytoa solution to the problem
⚠️ Watch out: by = not later than; until = up to that time. “Submit by Friday” ≠ “Submit until Friday”.

7 Conjunctions — The Logic Signals

Conjunctions join ideas and tell you the relationship between them. In IELTS Reading, they are the fastest way to answer True / False / Not Given and Matching Features questions.

LogicConjunctions / connectorsWhat it tells you
Additionand, also, moreover, furthermore, in additionmore of the same → answer is nearby
Contrastbut, however, although, whereas, nevertheless, despitethe opposite → check for NOT
Causebecause, since, due to, as a result ofthe reason → look before/after
Effectso, therefore, consequently, thusthe result
Conditionif, unless, provided thata limit on the statement
Timewhen, while, before, after, until, as soon assequence → useful for timelines
Examplefor example, such as, includinga specific case
The study was small, but the results were promising. (contrast — both parts matter)
It rained heavily; however, the match continued.
💡 IELTS use: if the passage says “although X, Y”, the statement “X caused Y” is probably False — although signals contrast, not cause.

8 Determiners — Articles & Quantifiers

Determiners come before a noun and specify it. They are the most common source of wrong answers in summary completion and sentence completion.

TypeWordsRule
Articlesa, an, thea/an = one non-specific; the = specific
Demonstrativesthis, that, these, thosenear / far, singular / plural
Possessivesmy, your, his, her, its, our, theirownership before a noun
Quantifiers — countablemany, few, a few, several, bothplural nouns
Quantifiers — uncountablemuch, little, a little, a great deal ofsingular uncountable nouns
Quantifiers — bothsome, any, no, all, most, plenty of, a lot ofcountable and uncountable
There isn't much evidence. (uncountable)
Many students passed. (countable plural)
⚠️ Watch out: if the gap-fill says “a ___ number of students”, the answer must be an adjective (large / small / significant), not a quantifier. Read what surrounds the gap.

9 Word Families — The IELTS Superpower

One root can produce four forms. IELTS paraphrases by changing the word family: the passage says “the city grew rapidly” and the question says “rapid growth”. If you know the family, you see the match.

VerbNounAdjectiveAdverb
growgrowthgrowing—
analyseanalysisanalyticalanalytically
concludeconclusionconclusiveconclusively
succeedsuccesssuccessfulsuccessfully
decidedecisiondecisivedecisively
createcreation / creativitycreativecreatively
informinformationinformativeinformatively
relyreliabilityreliablereliably
differdifferencedifferentdifferently
efficientefficiencyefficientefficiently
Passage: “The city grew rapidly.” → Question: “rapid growth” ✓
Passage: “Researchers analysed the data.” → Question: “the analysis of the data” ✓
💡 How to practise: take any passage and write the four forms of every key word. That single habit lifts both Reading and Writing scores.

10 Prefixes — Change the Meaning

Prefixes go before the root and change meaning, not word class. IELTS uses them heavily in True/False/Not Given and in Listening distractors.

PrefixMeaningExamples
un-notunfair, unusual, unclear
in- / im- / il- / ir-notinaccurate, impossible, illegal, irregular
dis-opposite / notdisagree, dishonest, disappear
non-notnon-fiction, non-renewable
mis-wronglymisunderstand, mislead
re-againrewrite, reconsider, rebuild
pre- / post-before / afterpre-war, postpone
over- / under-too much / too littleoverestimate, underestimate
inter- / intra-between / withininternational, intravenous
co- / sub- / super-together / under / aboveco-author, submarine, supernatural
⚠️ Watch out: the same prefix can attach to only some roots. “unpossible” ✗ → “impossible” ✓. Learn the collocation, not just the prefix.

11 Suffixes — Change the Word Class

Suffixes go after the root and change the word class. This is the single most useful table for IELTS gap-fills.

Target classCommon suffixesExamples
Noun-tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ance, -ence, -ship, -hood, -ism, -er, -or, -ist, -ageinformation, movement, happiness, ability, performance, friendship, teacher, actor
Verb-ise / -ize, -ify, -en, -atemodernise, simplify, strengthen, activate
Adjective-able, -ible, -al, -ive, -ous, -ful, -less, -ic, -y, -ent, -antreliable, visible, natural, creative, dangerous, careful, hopeless, historic, sunny, different
Adverb-lyquickly, carefully, unfortunately
The construction (noun) → to construct (verb) → constructive (adjective)
💡 How to use it: look at the word before the gap and the word after the gap. “a ___ decision” → adjective. “the ___ of the plan” → noun.

12 IELTS Answer-Type Prediction

Before you listen or scan, decide what kind of word must go in the gap. This one habit can add a full band to your Listening and Reading scores.

Gap positionWord class neededExample
After a / an / theNouna museum
After to (infinitive)Verbto register
After be (is / was / are)Adjective or nounis free / is a library
After an adverb like very / extremelyAdjective or adverbvery cold / very quickly
Before ofNounthe cost of the tour
After a verbNoun (object) or adverbcollected the tickets / ran quickly
At the start of a sentence + commaAdverb (connector)Unfortunately, …
After perNounper person
“The tour costs £15 per person.” → after per → noun
“The museum is free on Sundays.” → after is → adjective
⚠️ Watch out: a word can change class. Book is a noun in “a book” but a verb in “to book a table”. Always look at the surrounding grammar, not just the word.
📌
The 4-step IELTS routine: (1) Read the gap and the words around it. (2) Decide the word class. (3) Predict the meaning area (number? place? job?). (4) Listen or scan for a paraphrase — the answer is rarely the exact word in the question.

IELTS Reading & Listening Strategies

R Reading — How Parts of Speech Unlock Answers

Question typeHow word class helpsExample
True / False / Not GivenCheck the qualifier — adverbs like all, always, only, mainly decide the answer.“All participants agreed” vs passage “most” → False
Matching HeadingsMatch the noun phrase in the heading to the topic noun of the paragraph.Heading: “The decline of a species” → paragraph about falling numbers
Summary CompletionDecide the word class of each gap before looking for the word.“a ___ of the results” → noun
Matching InformationUse pronoun reference to follow a chain of ideas.“This discovery” → back to the previous sentence
Sentence EndingsThe ending must be grammatically compatible with the stem.Stem ending in “because” → an ending starting with a clause, not a noun
Multiple ChoiceReject options with the wrong word class or wrong qualifier strength.Option says “proves” but passage says “suggests” → wrong
💡 Paraphrase radar: when you find the keyword in the passage, read the whole sentence around it — the answer is usually a word family shift away.

L Listening — How to Predict the Answer

SectionTypical answer classWhat to predict
Section 1 — form / table completionNames, numbers, dates, addresses, pricesSpelling of names, digit-by-digit numbers
Section 2 — map / plan labellingPlaces + prepositions“opposite / next to / at the end of”
Section 3 — multiple choice / matchingOpinions, reasonsAdjectives and adverbs of judgement
Section 4 — note completionNouns, verbs, adjectivesWord class + meaning area
⚠️ Classic Listening traps:
• Self-correction: “It's £15 — no, sorry, £50.” → the second number is the answer.
• Distractor words: “We used to open at 9, but now it's 10.” → 10 is the answer.
• Spelling: names are spelled letter by letter — write as you hear, then check.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does knowing parts of speech help in IELTS Listening?

In gap-fill questions you can decide the word class of the answer before you listen (noun, verb, adjective, adverb). You then know what kind of word to catch — a name, a number, a place, a job — instead of trying to catch everything. It also warns you about word limits and spelling.

What is paraphrase and why does it matter in Reading?

Paraphrase means saying the same thing with different words — often by changing the word family or using synonyms. IELTS Reading questions almost never repeat the passage word-for-word. If the passage says “the city grew rapidly”, the question may say “rapid growth”. Spotting the family shift is the key skill.

Which word classes are most common in gap-fill answers?

Nouns are by far the most common answer type (names, places, dates, prices, objects), followed by verbs and adjectives. Adverbs appear mostly in sentence-completion and summary tasks. That is why Rule 1 and Rule 12 are the two most important rules on this page.

How do I deal with a word that can be more than one part of speech?

Look at the surrounding grammar, not the word itself. Book is a noun after “a” (“a book”) but a verb after “to” (“to book a table”). Fast is an adjective before a noun (“a fast car”) but an adverb after a verb (“he runs fast”). The position tells you the class.

Should I learn prefixes and suffixes for IELTS?

Yes — suffixes especially. They let you turn one known word into four (e.g. construct → construction → constructive → constructively). In both Reading and Listening, this is what lets you recognise the answer even when the exact word in the question is not in the passage.
