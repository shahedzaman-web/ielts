What Is Passive Voice — and Why IELTS Loves It

In the active voice, the subject does the action. In the passive voice, the subject receives the action — the doer may be mentioned with by or omitted entirely. IELTS examiners reward accurate passive use in Task 1 process descriptions and formal Task 2 essays.

💡 Core transformation: move the object to the subject position, change the verb to be + past participle, and optionally add by + doer.

Active: The chef cooked the meal. → Passive: The meal was cooked by the chef.
Active: They build houses. → Passive: Houses are built.
VoiceFocusIELTS use
ActiveWho does whatTask 2 arguments, strong opinions, personal examples
PassiveWhat happens to the receiverTask 1 processes/maps, academic hedging in Task 2
MixedBoth — normal essaysBand 7+: deliberate variation, not accidental

The 15 Rules — In Detail

Every rule includes the form, IELTS-specific examples, and the traps that cost band points.

1 The Basic Formula — be + Past Participle

Passive = form of “be” + past participle. The “be” changes with tense; the past participle (V3) never changes.

ActivePassive
The council builds the road.The road is built.
The council built the road.The road was built.
The council has built the road.The road has been built.
The council will build the road.The road will be built.
⚠️ Watch out: the past participle is the third form of the verb — written, taken, made, done, built, sent — not the past simple.

2 Passive Across All Tenses

TenseActivePassive
Present simpleThey clean the streets.The streets are cleaned.
Present continuousThey are cleaning the streets.The streets are being cleaned.
Present perfectThey have cleaned the streets.The streets have been cleaned.
Past simpleThey cleaned the streets.The streets were cleaned.
Past continuousThey were cleaning the streets.The streets were being cleaned.
Past perfectThey had cleaned the streets.The streets had been cleaned.
Future simpleThey will clean the streets.The streets will be cleaned.
Future perfectThey will have cleaned the streets.The streets will have been cleaned.
Modal (can / must / should)They must clean the streets.The streets must be cleaned.
⚠️ Not passive: perfect continuous tenses (e.g. “has been being cleaned”) are avoided in natural English — rewrite them instead.

3 When to Use Passive — and When NOT To

✅ Use it when

• the doer is unknown or obvious
• the action matters more than the doer
• writing Task 1 processes / maps
• Task 2 academic hedging (“It is argued that…”)

❌ Avoid it when

• the sentence becomes vague (“Mistakes were made”)
• the verb is intransitive (no passive)
• you’re overusing it — IELTS penalises monotony
• Task 1 bar/line charts: active is usually clearer

✅ The road was widened to reduce congestion. (doer unknown/unimportant)
✅ It is often argued that governments should invest more in public transport.
❌ It was decided that action would be taken. (vague — who decided?)
💡 Band 7 rule: use passive deliberately, not as a default. A clear active sentence beats a vague passive every time.

4 The “by” Agent — Include or Omit

Include by + doer only when the doer adds useful information. If the doer is unknown, obvious, or unimportant, omit it.

✅ The novel was written by Orwell. (useful — identifies the author)
✅ The bridge was built in 1890. (by whom is unimportant)
❌ The bridge was built by builders. (redundant — who else?)
💡 Task 1: in process descriptions, the agent is almost always omitted — “The beans are washed, then roasted…”

5 Passive with Modal Verbs

Modal + be + past participle. The modal never changes the passive form.

ActivePassive
We can solve the problem.The problem can be solved.
Governments must protect wildlife.Wildlife must be protected.
They should ban plastic bags.Plastic bags should be banned.
We might reduce emissions.Emissions might be reduced.
You could improve the system.The system could be improved.
✅ More funding should be allocated to public transport.
⚠️ Watch out: never double the modal — “must can be done” ✗ → “must be done” ✓.

6 Impersonal Passive — “It is believed that…”

This is the single most useful passive structure in Task 2. It lets you present opinions without writing “I think”, which IELTS academic writing prefers to avoid.

Reporting verbImpersonal passive
People think…It is thought that…
People believe…It is believed that…
People argue…It is argued that…
Researchers have found…It has been found that…
Experts suggest…It is suggested that…
Many people say…It is often said that…
✅ It is widely believed that education is the key to social mobility.
✅ It has been argued that stricter laws would reduce crime.
💡 Alternative: “Education is widely believed to be the key to social mobility.” — subject-to-infinitive passive. Also band 7+.

7 Causative Passive — have / get something done

Use have/get + object + past participle when someone else does the action for you.

StructureMeaningExample
have something donearrange for someone to do itI had my car repaired.
get something doneinformal versionShe got her hair cut.
have someone do somethingactive causativeI had the mechanic repair my car.
✅ Many cities have their waste processed in specialised plants. (Task 1 process)
⚠️ Watch out: do not confuse with “have done”. “I had my hair cut” (someone else cut it) ≠ “I had cut my hair” (past perfect — I cut it myself).

8 Passive with Two Objects

Verbs like give, send, offer, show, teach, tell, lend, pay can take two objects. In the passive, either object can become the subject — but the person is usually preferred.

ActivePassive (person first)Passive (thing first)
They gave him a medal.He was given a medal.A medal was given to him.
They sent us the documents.We were sent the documents.The documents were sent to us.
They offered her a job.She was offered a job.A job was offered to her.
They taught the students grammar.The students were taught grammar.Grammar was taught to the students.
💡 IELTS: “People are often given very little choice” is more natural than “Very little choice is often given to people”.

9 Passive in Task 1 — Process Diagrams

Process descriptions are 80% passive. The doer is usually the factory or the process itself, so the agent is omitted. Focus on sequencing words + passive verbs.

✅ First, the coffee beans are harvested. They are then dried in the sun and roasted at high temperatures. Finally, the beans are ground and packaged for distribution.
Sequencing wordPassive verb example
First / Initially… is/are collected
Next / Then / After that… is/are transported
Following this… is/are processed
Finally / Lastly… is/are distributed
⚠️ Watch out: don’t mix active and passive mid-process (“The beans are harvested, then workers dry them, then they are roasted”) — choose passive throughout for consistency.

10 Passive in Task 2 — Academic Tone

Task 2 uses passive mainly for hedging (avoiding certainty) and impersonal argument (avoiding “I”).

FunctionExample
Stating common beliefIt is widely believed that…
Presenting researchIt has been shown that…
Proposing actionMore resources should be allocated to…
Describing policyStricter laws must be enforced to…
Stating a general truthChildren are often exposed to…
Describing a process/phenomenonWaste is generated at an alarming rate.
✅ It is often argued that governments should be held accountable for environmental damage.
💡 Balance: a Band 7+ Task 2 essay mixes both. Aim for roughly 2–3 passive constructions per paragraph, not more.

11 Intransitive Verbs — No Passive!

Intransitive verbs have no object, so they cannot be made passive.

VerbCorrect (active)Wrong (passive)
happenAn accident happened.An accident was happened ✗
arriveThey arrived late.They were arrived ✗
occurThe change occurred suddenly.The change was occurred ✗
dieThe plant died.The plant was died ✗
appearA problem appeared.A problem was appeared ✗
risePrices rose.Prices were risen ✗
⚠️ Common IELTS error: “The number of cars was increased” is fine (increase is transitive), but “Prices were risen” is wrong (rise is intransitive). Use raise for transitive: “The government raised prices” → “Prices were raised”.

12 Common Passive Mistakes

❌ Wrong✅ RightWhy
The book was wrote by Orwell.The book was written by Orwell.Past participle, not past simple.
The house is build every year.The house is built every year.Past participle, not base verb.
The problem can solved.The problem can be solved.Missing “be”.
It was happened last night.It happened last night.“Happen” is intransitive.
The car was repairing yesterday.The car was repaired yesterday.-ing form ≠ passive.
They were given to him a medal.He was given a medal.Word order in two-object passive.

13 Passive in Conditionals and Complex Structures

Passive works inside conditionals, relative clauses and reduced clauses.

✅ If stricter laws were introduced, crime would fall. (second conditional, passive)
✅ If the report had been submitted earlier, the decision would have been made. (third conditional, passive)
✅ The policy, which was implemented in 2019, has failed. (relative clause, passive)
✅ Once collected, the waste is sorted. (reduced passive clause)
💡 Task 2 use: passive conditionals let you discuss hypothetical policy without naming who would do it — perfect for “should governments…” essays.

14 Active vs Passive — the Band-Score Balance

IELTS examiners penalise overuse of passive as much as underuse. Aim for a natural mix.

BandTypical passive useScore effect
Band 5Mostly active, with occasional incorrect passiveGrammar errors limit the score
Band 6Passive used but sometimes clumsy or overdoneSome flexibility, but errors persist
Band 7Accurate passive in processes and hedging; active where clearerRange and accuracy rewarded
Band 8–9Deliberate, varied, and stylistically controlledNatural voice with precise grammar
💡 Rule of thumb: in Task 1 process essays, use passive for the steps; in bar charts, use active for “increased / decreased”. In Task 2, use passive for hedging, active for opinions and examples.

15 Passive in Task 1 — Maps and Changes

Maps describe changes over time — often with passive verbs because the doer (council, planners) is obvious or unknown.

VerbTypical passive use
build / constructA new bridge was built in 1990.
demolish / knock downThe old factory was demolished.
widen / expandThe road was widened.
convert / transformThe warehouse was converted into a museum.
replaceThe trees were replaced by a car park.
developThe area was developed for housing.
✅ In 1990, a new shopping centre was constructed on the site, and the old school was demolished to make way for a car park.
📌
The IELTS passive routine: (1) Ask “does the doer matter here?” (2) If not — use passive. (3) Check the tense — be must match. (4) Check the participle — V3, not V2. (5) Check intransitive verbs — no passive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to use passive voice in IELTS Writing Task 1?

Only in process diagrams and (partly) in map descriptions, where the doer is unknown or unimportant. In line graphs, bar charts and tables you should mostly use active verbs (“sales increased”, “the UK produced the most steel”). Passive is not required — accurate, natural grammar is what earns marks.

How much passive is “too much” in Task 2?

If every sentence is passive, the essay sounds vague and monotonous — examiners penalise this as a lack of grammatical range. Aim for 2–3 accurate passive constructions per paragraph, mostly for hedging (“It is argued that…”) and policy statements (“Stricter laws should be enforced”). Use active for opinions and examples.

What is the difference between “was built” and “has been built”?

“Was built” = past simple passive — the building was constructed at a finished past point (e.g. “The bridge was built in 1890”). “Has been built” = present perfect passive — the construction is now complete, and the result is relevant now (e.g. “A new bridge has been built to reduce traffic”).

Why is “an accident was happened” wrong?

Happen is an intransitive verb — it has no object, so it cannot be made passive. The correct form is simply “An accident happened.” Common intransitive verbs in IELTS include happen, occur, arrive, appear, rise, die, come, go.

What is the best impersonal passive construction for Task 2?

“It is [verb] that…” is the most useful pattern: It is argued / believed / thought / suggested / widely accepted that… It lets you present common views without writing “I think”, which academic IELTS Writing prefers to avoid. You can also use the subject-to-infinitive passive: “Education is widely believed to be the key to success.”
