YOUR ANSWER, CORRECTED

Your ideas, your structure and your sentences, with the errors fixed. Three things were added: a clearer thesis, a specific example in Body 1, and a "However..." sentence at the end of Body 2. With these changes, this essay would score about Band 7.

Green text = what was changed or added. Everything else is your own writing.

Many individuals argue that the most important quality of a good leader is the ability to listen to others, while others contend that it is more important for a leader to make quick and firm decisions. In my opinion, listening is the more important quality, because good decisions depend on understanding people.

On the one hand, there are several reasons why a leader needs to listen to others. A significant reason is that it encourages cooperation and builds trust. For instance, when a manager listens to employees' opinions and problems carefully, staff are more likely to share their concerns with him or her. This builds trust: people feel more secure and receive emotional support. As a result, the leader can solve problems more effectively and provide them with useful advice. If a leader cannot pay attention to people's needs, then it is very difficult to build a balanced society and strong relationships.

On the other hand, the ability to make quick and firm decisions is also beneficial for a leader. The primary reason is that they show confidence and competence. For instance, a leader needs to deal with different kinds of challenges and establish fair rules. If they are able to make decisions instantly, it saves time and increases productivity. What's more, quick and firm decisions allow a leader to take advantage of opportunities before their competitors. A decisive leader can maintain discipline and prevent unnecessary arguments among team members. However, a quick decision made without listening to others can easily be the wrong one, so decisiveness is most valuable when it is based on what the leader has heard.

In conclusion, although both sides present valid arguments, I contend that a good leader must have the ability to listen to others because it fosters collaboration, provides emotional support and makes their decisions wiser.

MODEL ANSWER (BAND 7.5–8)

Some people believe that a good leader's most important quality is the ability to listen, while others feel that making quick, firm decisions matters more. In my view, although decisiveness is vital in emergencies, listening is the more fundamental quality because it makes good decisions possible.

Those who prioritise listening argue that leaders cannot solve problems they do not understand. By consulting citizens, employees or experts, a leader gains a clearer picture of people's real needs and is less likely to introduce policies that fail. Listening also builds trust: people are far more willing to follow a leader who has taken their concerns seriously. For example, a mayor who holds public meetings before redesigning a city's transport system is likely to face less opposition than one who imposes changes without warning.

On the other hand, there are situations where hesitation can be costly. During the COVID-19 pandemic, governments that imposed lockdowns and travel restrictions early were often more successful in limiting the spread of the virus than those that delayed. In such crises, endless consultation can waste precious time, and a leader must be willing to act firmly even when the information is incomplete.

However, I believe that quick decisions are only valuable when they are informed ones. A leader who acts rapidly but ignores expert advice may make serious mistakes, as a poorly planned emergency measure can cause as much harm as the problem itself. The best leaders, therefore, listen continuously so that, when a crisis arrives, they are ready to decide quickly and wisely.

In conclusion, both qualities are important, but listening is the foundation of effective leadership, while decisiveness is most essential in emergencies.
