What Is a Verb Tense?

Verb tenses are changes or additions to verbs to show when an action took place: in the past, present, or future. They also include grammatical aspects that add details about duration or completion.

💡 Core Idea: English has 3 main tenses (past, present, future) × 4 aspects (simple, perfect, continuous, perfect continuous) = 12 verb tenses.

🔸 Simple

Basic form, no extra information.

I help.

🔸 Perfect

Completed actions relating to another time.

I have helped.

🔸 Continuous

Ongoing actions in progress.

I am helping.

🔸 Perfect Continuous

Ongoing actions completed up to a point.

I have been helping.

Usage Rules for Speaking & Writing

Knowing the forms is only half the battle. These are the usage rules that tell you which tense to pick in real conversation and real writing.

C Stative Verbs — Never Use Continuous

Some verbs describe states, not actions. They normally do not take continuous forms — even when the meaning is “now”.

GroupVerbsExample
Thinkingknow, believe, understand, remember, forget, mean, doubt, supposeI understand the question.
Emotionslike, love, hate, want, need, prefer, mind, careShe wants a coffee.
Sensessee, hear, smell, taste, feel (perception)I see the mountain.
Possessionhave (=own), own, belong, possessHe has two cars.
Appearanceseem, look, appear, resemble, cost, weighIt seems fine.

💡 Some can go continuous when the meaning changes:

I am thinking about it. (= considering — action)
I am having lunch. (= eating — action)
She is seeing a doctor. (= consulting — action)
He is tasting the soup. (= sampling — action)

D Time Clauses — No Future After When / If / Before / After

After time and conditional conjunctions (when, if, before, after, as soon as, until, while, by the time), use a present tense — not will — even when you mean the future.

✅ I'll call you when I arrive. (not will arrive)
✅ We'll leave as soon as she gets here.
✅ If it rains, we'll stay home.
✅ After he finishes work, he'll come over.
❌ I'll call you when I will arrive.

E Conditionals — The Four Patterns

TypeIf-clauseMain clauseExample
Zero (facts)present simplepresent simpleIf you heat ice, it melts.
First (real future)present simplewill + baseIf it rains, we will stay home.
Second (unreal present)past simplewould + baseIf I had money, I would travel.
Third (unreal past)past perfectwould have + past participleIf she had studied, she would have passed.
Mixedpast perfectwould + base (now)If I had saved money, I would be rich now.
💡 Wish + past / past perfect: I wish I had more time. / I wish I had studied harder.

F Reported Speech — Backshift One Step

When you report what someone said, move the tense one step back into the past.

Direct speechReported speech
“I am tired.”She said she was tired.
“I work here.”He said he worked there.
“I will come.”She said she would come.
“I have finished.”He said he had finished.
“I can swim.”She said she could swim.
“I went home.”He said he had gone home.
⚠️ No backshift when the reporting verb is in the present: “She says she is tired.”

G will vs going to

SituationUseExample
Spontaneous decisionwillI will have the soup.
Plan / intention before speakinggoing toI am going to visit my aunt.
Promise / offerwillI will help you.
Prediction based on evidencegoing toLook at those clouds — it is going to rain.
Prediction based on opinionwillI think she will win.
Formal announcementwillThe company will open a new branch.

H Present Tenses for the Future

English often uses present forms to talk about the future — especially in speech.

Present Continuous for fixed arrangements: I am meeting her at 6.
Simple Present for timetables: The train leaves at 7:30.
Present Continuous for near future: We are having dinner tonight.
⚠️ Do not use present for future after when / if — use present with future meaning: “When she arrives, we'll eat.”

I Past Forms for Politeness & Hypotheticals

Past forms don't always mean past. They also make requests more polite and situations more hypothetical.

I was wondering if you could help. (polite)
Could you open the window? (polite request)
If I had a car, I would drive. (hypothetical present)
I wish I knew the answer. (unreal present)

J Common Errors — Wrong vs Right

❌ Wrong✅ RightWhy
I have seen him yesterday.I saw him yesterday.“Yesterday” is finished time → simple past.
Did you went to the party?Did you go to the party?After did, use the base verb.
She is knowing the answer.She knows the answer.Know is stative — no continuous.
I am living here since 2010.I have lived here since 2010.“Since” + unfinished time → present perfect.
When I will arrive, I'll call.When I arrive, I'll call.No future after when.
He has went home.He has gone home.Past participle, not past simple.
They was playing.They were playing.Plural subject → were.
I will have been here since 2020.I have been here since 2020.“Since” up to now → present perfect.
📌
How to choose a tense: (1) Is it past, present, or future? (2) Is the action finished or continuing? (3) Is it a state verb? (4) Is there a signal word? (5) Is it a time clause or conditional? Answer those five questions and the tense will almost always pick itself.

Past Tenses

Actions that happened before now.

Simple Past

[verb + -ed] or irregular

Completed actions in the past, with no extra emphasis.

I helped my neighbor yesterday.
I went to the store this morning.
Past Perfect

[had] + [past participle]

One past action happened before another past action.

She had arrived before she realized it was Sunday.
I ran to my car when I noticed my wife had left.
Past Continuous

[was/were] + [present participle]

Ongoing action in the past, often interrupted.

My dog was whimpering when the TV woke him.
As kids, we were always getting into trouble.
Past Perfect Continuous

[had] + [been] + [present participle]

Ongoing past actions that happened before another past action.

Before his first job, he had been working as a proofreader.
I had been living on my friend's couch for a year.
🔆

Present Tenses

Actions happening now or general truths.

Simple Present

root verb (add -s for he/she/it)

Habitual actions, general truths, or actions now.

I feel like a million bucks!
My brother carries the groceries.
Present Perfect

[have/has] + [past participle]

Actions started in the past with a present connection.

We have tricked him every April Fool's Day.
My niece has grown so much!
Present Continuous

[am/is/are] + [present participle]

Actions happening right now or in the near future.

I am reading The Hitchhiker's Guide.
We are eating pizza tonight.
Present Perfect Continuous

[have/has] + [been] + [present participle]

Ongoing actions started in the past, still happening.

We have been waiting for over an hour!
The team has been practicing nonstop.
🚀

Future Tenses

Actions that will happen later.

Simple Future

[will] + [root verb]

Actions that have not happened yet but will later.

She will be president one day.
I will not go without a date!
Future Perfect

[will] + [have] + [past participle]

An action that will be completed by a specified future time.

By the time you read this, I will have left.
She will have eaten before her sister wakes.
Future Continuous

[will] + [be] + [present participle]

Future actions happening over a period of time.

This time tomorrow, I will be drinking margaritas.
We will be attending a meeting from noon to 3.
Future Perfect Continuous

[will] + [have] + [been] + [present participle]

Future ongoing actions that continue up until a certain point.

In ten minutes, my parents will have been waiting for four hours.
I will have been eating healthy for a year by September.
