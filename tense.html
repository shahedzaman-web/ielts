What Is a Verb Tense?

Verb tenses are changes or additions to verbs to show when an action took place: in the past, present, or future. They also include grammatical aspects that add details about duration or completion.

💡 Core Idea: English has 3 main tenses (past, present, future) × 4 aspects (simple, perfect, continuous, perfect continuous) = 12 verb tenses.

🔸 Simple

Basic form, no extra information.

I help.

🔸 Perfect

Completed actions relating to another time.

I have helped.

🔸 Continuous

Ongoing actions in progress.

I am helping.

🔸 Perfect Continuous

Ongoing actions completed up to a point.

I have been helping.

Past Tenses

Actions that happened before now.

Simple Past

[verb + -ed] or irregular

Completed actions in the past, with no extra emphasis.

I helped my neighbor yesterday.
I went to the store this morning.
Past Perfect

[had] + [past participle]

One past action happened before another past action.

She had arrived before she realized it was Sunday.
I ran to my car when I noticed my wife had left.
Past Continuous

[was/were] + [present participle]

Ongoing action in the past, often interrupted.

My dog was whimpering when the TV woke him.
As kids, we were always getting into trouble.
Past Perfect Continuous

[had] + [been] + [present participle]

Ongoing past actions that happened before another past action.

Before his first job, he had been working as a proofreader.
I had been living on my friend's couch for a year.
🔆

Present Tenses

Actions happening now or general truths.

Simple Present

root verb (add -s for he/she/it)

Habitual actions, general truths, or actions now.

I feel like a million bucks!
My brother carries the groceries.
Present Perfect

[have/has] + [past participle]

Actions started in the past with a present connection.

We have tricked him every April Fool's Day.
My niece has grown so much!
Present Continuous

[am/is/are] + [present participle]

Actions happening right now or in the near future.

I am reading The Hitchhiker's Guide.
We are eating pizza tonight.
Present Perfect Continuous

[have/has] + [been] + [present participle]

Ongoing actions started in the past, still happening.

We have been waiting for over an hour!
The team has been practicing nonstop.
🚀

Future Tenses

Actions that will happen later.

Simple Future

[will] + [root verb]

Actions that have not happened yet but will later.

She will be president one day.
I will not go without a date!
Future Perfect

[will] + [have] + [past participle]

An action that will be completed by a specified future time.

By the time you read this, I will have left.
She will have eaten before her sister wakes.
Future Continuous

[will] + [be] + [present participle]

Future actions happening over a period of time.

This time tomorrow, I will be drinking margaritas.
We will be attending a meeting from noon to 3.
Future Perfect Continuous

[will] + [have] + [been] + [present participle]

Future ongoing actions that continue up until a certain point.

In ten minutes, my parents will have been waiting for four hours.
I will have been eating healthy for a year by September.
