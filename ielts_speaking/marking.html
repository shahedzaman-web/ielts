📋 How Speaking Is Scored

Four equally-weighted criteria · Each scored 0–9 · Final band = average

Your IELTS Speaking score is made up of four equally-weighted criteria. Each criterion is scored from 0 to 9, and your overall Speaking band is the average of the four — rounded to the nearest half-band. This means you cannot ignore any single criterion; each is worth 25% of your total score.

Criterion Weight What It Measures Band 7 Target
🗣️ Fluency & Coherence 25% Flow of speech, natural pausing, logical development, use of discourse markers Speaks at length without noticeable effort; uses connectives flexibly
📚 Lexical Resource 25% Vocabulary range, precision, collocations, idioms, paraphrasing ability Flexible vocabulary; some less common items; effective paraphrase
⚙️ Grammatical Range & Accuracy 25% Variety of sentence structures, tense control, error frequency Range of complex structures; frequent error-free sentences
🔊 Pronunciation 25% Individual sounds, word stress, sentence stress, intonation, chunking Generally clear; some accent but easily understood throughout

📊 The Four Criteria — In Detail

Band descriptors · Improvement strategies · Practice drills · Pitfalls

🗣️

Fluency & Coherence

⚖️ 25% of total score
Band Descriptors
Band 7 Speaks at length without noticeable effort or loss of coherence. May demonstrate language-related hesitation at times, or some repetition and/or self-correction. Uses a range of connectives and discourse markers with some flexibility.
Band 8 Speaks fluently with only occasional repetition or self-correction. Hesitation is usually content-related rather than language-related. Develops topics coherently and appropriately. Uses a wide range of connectives and discourse markers naturally.
How to Improve
1 Use the 4–3–2 drill. Pick one topic and speak about it for 4 minutes, then 3 minutes, then 2 minutes. Each round forces faster retrieval and reduces hesitation. Do this daily with different Part 2 topics.
2 Master discourse markers. Learn and use a range: To begin with, Having said that, On the other hand, As far as I'm concerned, That said, Ultimately. Group them by function (adding, contrasting, concluding, exemplifying).
3 Replace disruptive fillers. Swap um, uh, like with natural thinking phrases: That's an interesting question…, Let me think about that for a moment…, How shall I put this…?
4 Pause at idea boundaries. Pause naturally between sentences and clauses, not mid-phrase. This signals confidence and makes you easier to follow.
5 Record and review. Listen for long pauses, repeated words, and moments where you lose your train of thought. Note the topics that cause the most hesitation and prepare vocabulary for them.

🎯 Weekly Practice Drill

Day 1–3: 4–3–2 drill with one Part 2 topic per day.
Day 4–5: Record yourself answering 5 Part 1 questions in under 45 seconds each — no pauses longer than 2 seconds.
Day 6–7: Full mock Part 2 (2 minutes) + Part 3 discussion (5 minutes). Review recording for hesitations.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

  • Memorising full answers — sounds stiff and breaks natural flow
  • Overusing filler sounds (um, uh, like) instead of thinking phrases
  • Pausing mid-sentence because you're searching for the "perfect" word
  • Speaking too fast and losing coherence just to appear fluent
  • Ignoring the examiner's follow-up questions because you're focused on your script
📚

Lexical Resource

⚖️ 25% of total score
Band Descriptors
Band 7 Uses vocabulary resource flexibly to discuss a variety of topics. Uses some less common and idiomatic vocabulary, showing some awareness of style and collocation, with some inappropriate choices. Uses paraphrase effectively as required.
Band 8 Uses a wide vocabulary resource readily and flexibly to convey precise meaning. Uses less common and idiomatic vocabulary skilfully, with occasional inaccuracies. Uses paraphrase effectively as required.
How to Improve
1 Build topic-specific word banks. For each Part 3 topic (housing, education, technology, environment, etc.), collect 8–10 precise terms with collocations. Don't learn isolated words — learn them in phrases.
2 Master collocations. Learn which words naturally go together: make a decision (not do a decision), heavy traffic (not strong traffic), highly unlikely (not strongly unlikely).
3 Practise paraphrasing. Take a simple sentence and rewrite it three ways using synonyms and different structures. E.g., "I like reading" → "I'm fond of reading" → "Reading is something I really enjoy" → "I have a strong preference for reading."
4 Use idioms selectively. 1–2 idioms per answer is enough. Force them only when they fit naturally. A well-placed idiom is far more impressive than three awkward ones.
5 Record and upgrade. After answering a question, transcribe your answer and replace common words with more precise alternatives. Build a personal "upgrade list."

🎯 Weekly Practice Drill

Day 1–2: Collocation mapping — take 10 common verbs (make, do, take, have, get) and list 5 natural collocations for each.
Day 3–4: Paraphrase practice — rewrite 10 simple sentences in three different ways.
Day 5–7: Topic vocabulary — pick 2 Part 3 topics, learn 10 collocations each, then answer 4 questions using at least 5 of them.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

  • Using advanced words incorrectly — vocabulary is not the same as lexicon in everyday speech
  • Forcing idioms that don't fit the context or tone
  • Learning word lists without collocations — isolated words rarely sound natural
  • Repeating the same word 4–5 times in one answer when a synonym exists
  • Using overly formal academic words in a conversational Part 1 answer
⚙️

Grammatical Range & Accuracy

⚖️ 25% of total score
Band Descriptors
Band 7 Uses a range of complex structures with some flexibility. Frequently produces error-free sentences, though some grammatical mistakes persist. Errors do not impede communication.
Band 8 Uses a wide range of structures flexibly. Produces a majority of error-free sentences with only occasional slips. Errors are minor and do not affect clarity.
How to Improve
1 Mix sentence types deliberately. In every answer, aim for at least one simple sentence, one compound sentence (with and, but, so), and one complex sentence (with because, although, which, if).
2 Practise conditionals in Part 3. Part 3 questions often invite hypothetical answers. Use "If the government were to invest more…, it could…" and "Provided that…, people would…" naturally.
3 Master past tenses for Part 2. Most Part 2 tasks describe past experiences. Drill past simple, past continuous, past perfect, and used to until they are automatic.
4 Use relative clauses. Add natural detail with who, which, that, where: "I visited a place which was famous for its tea gardens" instead of two short sentences.
5 Self-correct gracefully. If you make a mistake, correct it immediately and move on: "He go — he goes to work by bus." Do not restart the whole sentence.

🎯 Weekly Practice Drill

Day 1–2: Sentence transformation — take 10 simple sentences and rewrite each as compound and complex.
Day 3–4: Tense drill — describe your day (past), your routine (present), and your plans (future) in 90 seconds each.
Day 5–7: Conditional practice — answer 6 Part 3 questions using at least one conditional per answer.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

  • Forcing every sentence to be complex — this creates errors and sounds unnatural
  • Mixing tenses within the same idea (e.g., switching from past to present mid-story)
  • Subject-verb agreement errors — "The people in my country is…"
  • Using "discuss about" (incorrect) instead of "discuss" (correct)
  • Overusing "very" and "a lot of" instead of varied structures
🔊

Pronunciation

⚖️ 25% of total score
Band Descriptors
Band 7 Generally clear pronunciation with some effective use of pronunciation features. Can be understood throughout, though individual words or sounds may occasionally be unclear. Accent has some effect on intelligibility but not enough to impede communication.
Band 8 Easy to understand throughout. Uses a wide range of pronunciation features with flexibility. Accent has minimal effect on intelligibility. Sustained flexible use of features is evident.
How to Improve
1 Shadowing. Listen to a short audio clip (30–60 seconds) from a native speaker. Play it, pause, and repeat sentence by sentence, imitating the speaker's rhythm, stress, and intonation. Do this for 10–15 minutes daily.
2 Word stress awareness. English word stress is fixed and can change meaning. Learn stress patterns when you learn new words: deVELopment (not DEvelopment), phoTOgraphy (not PHOtography).
3 Sentence stress. Stress the important content words and reduce function words. "I WANT to GO to the BEACH this WEEKEND." This creates natural English rhythm.
4 Chunking. Break speech into meaningful groups of words: "When I was a child | I used to play cricket | with my friends." This makes you easier to follow and sounds more fluent.
5 Record and compare. Record yourself reading a short passage. Listen back and compare with a native speaker's version. Focus on sounds you consistently mispronounce (e.g., v/w, th, long/short vowels).

🎯 Weekly Practice Drill

Day 1–2: Shadowing — 10 minutes daily with a podcast or YouTube clip. Focus on rhythm and intonation.
Day 3–4: Minimal pairs — practise confusing sound pairs (ship/sheep, very/wary, think/sink) for 10 minutes.
Day 5–7: Record a 90-second Part 2 answer, transcribe it, and mark where stress should fall. Re-record with correct stress.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

  • Speaking too quietly or mumbling — the examiner must hear you clearly
  • Flat intonation — English uses rising and falling tones to convey meaning
  • Pronouncing every word with equal stress instead of stressing content words
  • Confusing v and w — "very" not "wery"
  • Mispronouncing th sounds — "think" not "tink"
  • Using an accent that impedes understanding — clarity matters more than native-like accent

🗓️ 30-Day Improvement Plan

Balanced focus across all four criteria · 30–45 minutes daily

Days 1–10 · Foundation
  • FC: 4–3–2 drill, 10 min daily
  • LR: Build 2 topic word banks
  • GRA: Sentence transformation drills
  • P: Shadowing, 10 min daily
Days 11–20 · Development
  • FC: Record full Part 2 + Part 3
  • LR: Paraphrase practice + idioms
  • GRA: Conditional & relative clause drills
  • P: Minimal pairs + intonation work
Days 21–30 · Mastery
  • FC: Full mock tests, timed
  • LR: Cross-topic vocabulary revision
  • GRA: Error review from recordings
  • P: Final recording comparison
💡

Pro tip: After every recording, rate yourself 1–9 on each criterion using the band descriptors above. Track your scores in a notebook. You will notice which criterion needs the most attention and can adjust your practice accordingly.

💡 Quick Reference Tips

Small changes that make a big difference across all criteria

1Don't memorise scripts. Examiners detect rehearsed answers instantly. Learn structures and vocabulary, not fixed sentences.
2Extend every answer. Aim for 2–4 sentences in Part 1, 1.5–2 minutes in Part 2, and 35–60 seconds in Part 3.
3Use natural thinking phrases. "That's an interesting question…" buys you time without sounding hesitant.
4Self-correct without panic. If you make a mistake, correct it briefly and continue. Fluency is about recovery, not perfection.
5Use Bangladeshi examples naturally. Cox's Bazar, Dhaka traffic, Eid, monsoon, garment industry — these make answers authentic and easier to develop.
6Record yourself weekly. Listen for patterns in your errors — repeated grammar mistakes, overused words, unclear pronunciation.
7Practise under timed conditions. Use a timer for Part 2 (1 min prep, 2 min speak) and Part 3 (35–60 sec per answer).
8Focus on clarity, not accent. You don't need a British or American accent. You need to be clearly understood throughout.
