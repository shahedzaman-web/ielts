1. Simple Sentences

1.1 What is a Simple Sentence?

A simple sentence contains exactly one independent clause – one subject and one main verb. It expresses a complete thought and can stand alone.

Formula: [Subject] + [Verb] + (Object/Complement)
Examples Car sales increased.
The figure for London was the highest.
Renewable energy accounted for 30% of total production.

1.2 Simple Sentence Structures

StructureFormulaExample
Subject + VerbS + VSales increased.
Subject + Verb + ObjectS + V + OCar sales increased by 15%.
Subject + Verb + ComplementS + V + CCoal remained the primary source.
Subject + Verb + AdverbS + V + AdvPrices rose sharply.
Compound SubjectS1 + and + S2 + VThe UK and France recorded similar figures.
Compound PredicateS + V1 + and/but + V2The rate peaked in 2008 but dropped steadily thereafter.
In + Year StructureIn + Year + , + S + VIn 2008, Organization A stood at 0.5 billion dollars.

1.3 Loading Simple Sentences with Prepositions

LayerSentence
S + VSales increased.
+ WhereSales in the retail sector increased.
+ How muchSales in the retail sector increased by 15%.
+ WhenSales in the retail sector increased by 15% in the first quarter.
+ To whatSales in the retail sector increased by 15% in the first quarter to a total of $5 million.
Subject + Verb + Object + Modifier
The price rose sharply in 2020.
✅ When to Use Simple Sentences Simple sentences are ideal for:
  • Introducing a topic or key point
  • Stating a clear fact or data point
  • Providing an overview statement
  • Creating emphasis and clarity

2. Compound Sentences

2.1 What is a Compound Sentence?

A compound sentence joins two independent clauses of equal importance using a coordinating conjunction or a semicolon.

Formula: [Clause 1] + , + [conjunction] + [Clause 2]  or  [Clause 1] + ; + [Clause 2]

2.2 Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

ConjunctionFunctionExample
ForReasonHe stayed home, for he was ill.
AndAdditionCar production increased, and truck sales followed.
NorNegative additionHe didn't call, nor did he write.
ButContrastCoal remained dominant, but gas overtook it in 2005.
OrAlternativeYou can take the train, or you can drive.
YetContrast (formal)Profits fell, yet the company survived.
SoResultThe liquid is heated, so it evaporates.

2.3 Four Primary Compound Structures

The "And" Structure – Addition / Parallel Car production increased by 20% in 2010, and truck manufacturing followed a similar upward trajectory.

The first step involves sorting the materials, and the second phase requires crushing them.
The "But / Yet" Structure – Direct Contrast Coal remained the primary energy source in 1990, but it was overtaken by natural gas in 2005.

Desktop computer sales fell significantly, yet tablet sales remained relatively stable.
The "So" Structure – Result / Consequence The liquid is heated to 100 degrees Celsius, so it evaporates into a gas.

Funding for the project was cut in half, so the construction timeline was delayed.
The Semicolon – "Naked" Connection In 2000, Japan exported 50 million vehicles; Germany exported just 30 million.

Male employment rates peaked in the summer; female rates hit their lowest point.

2.4 Advanced: Semicolon + Conjunctive Adverb

Formula: [Clause 1] + ; + [Transition Word] + , + [Clause 2]
Transition WordFunctionExample
howeverContrastProfits grew steadily; however, Company B experienced severe fluctuations.
furthermoreAdditionSolar energy saw a 10% increase; furthermore, installation costs dropped.
thereforeResultThe population doubled; therefore, new infrastructure was built.
in contrastContrastGermany recorded 80%; in contrast, France recorded only 45%.
Independent Clause 1 , [conjunction] Independent Clause 2
Sales rose , but expenses fell.
❌ The "Comma Splice" Trap Incorrect: Beef consumption fell to 20 pounds, chicken consumption rose to 50 pounds. (Using a comma without a conjunction = Grammatical Error)
✅ Correction 1 (Conjunction): Beef consumption fell to 20 pounds, and chicken consumption rose to 50 pounds.
✅ Correction 2 (Semicolon): Beef consumption fell to 20 pounds; chicken consumption rose to 50 pounds.

3. Complex Sentences

3.1 What is a Complex Sentence?

A complex sentence connects one independent clause with one or more dependent clauses using a subordinating conjunction.

Formula: [Subordinating Conjunction] + [Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause]
            or
[Independent Clause] + , + [Subordinating Conjunction] + [Dependent Clause]

3.2 Common Subordinating Conjunctions

TypeConjunctionsExample
Contrastwhile, whereas, although, even thoughWhile oil dropped, gas grew.
Timebefore, after, when, as, once, untilAfter remaining stable, the population surged.
Reasonbecause, since, asBecause the price rose, demand fell.
Conditionif, unless, provided thatIf the mixture is not heated, the reaction fails.

3.3 Contrast Structures

While / Whereas (Direct Comparison) While oil consumption dropped to 20 million barrels in 2015, natural gas usage grew to 50 million barrels.
The UK invested heavily in renewable energy, whereas France focused primarily on nuclear power.
Although / Even Though (Surprising Contrast) Although coal was the primary energy source in 1990, it was heavily outpaced by natural gas by 2010.
Even though profits dipped in the second quarter, the company ended the year at a record high.

3.4 Time Sequence Structures

The "As" Pattern (Simultaneous Action) As the temperature of the water rises, the ice melts rapidly.
As the unemployment rate increased, consumer spending decreased proportionately.
The "Before / After" Pattern (Sequential Events) Sales peaked at 500 units in 2015 before dropping sharply the following year.
After remaining stable for an entire decade, the population surged by 20% in 2020.

3.5 Condition Structures

If / Unless (Conditional) If the mixture is not heated to exactly 100°C, the chemical reaction fails.
Unless the raw materials are crushed into a fine powder, they cannot be mixed with water.
[Subordinating Conjunction] + [Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause]
While car sales dropped, truck sales rose.
⚠️ The Golden Punctuation Rule Dependent Clause FIRST: Comma after dependent clause → While profits rose, expenses fell.
Independent Clause FIRST: Usually NO comma → Expenses fell while profits rose.

4. Compound-Complex Sentences

4.1 What is a Compound-Complex Sentence?

A compound-complex sentence contains at least two independent clauses (compound) AND at least one dependent clause (complex).

Formula: [Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause 1] + , and/but + [Independent Clause 2]

4.2 Three Essential Structures

Structure 1: The "Contrast + Addition" Formula: While/Although + [Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause 1] + , and/but + [Independent Clause 2].

While the number of male graduates dropped significantly, female graduate numbers rose to 50,000, and the figure for postgraduates remained perfectly stable.
Although housing accounted for the largest share of the budget in 2000, it was overtaken by food in 2010, but transportation costs stayed consistently low across both years.
Structure 2: The "Compound + Relative" Formula: [Independent Clause 1] + , but/and + [Independent Clause 2] + , which + [Relative Clause].

Coal consumption fell steadily throughout the decade, but natural gas usage surged to 40%, which was the highest figure recorded on the chart.
The UK spent $50 million on healthcare, and France spent a nearly identical amount, which represented a massive increase from the previous year.
Structure 3: The "Compound + Time Sequence" Formula: [Independent Clause 1] + , and + [Independent Clause 2] + before/after + [Dependent Time Clause].

Profits hit a peak of $1 million in 2015, and they remained at this level for exactly three years before dropping sharply in 2019.
The liquid is heated to 100 degrees, and it is passed through a filtration screen before it is bottled for distribution.
[Dependent Clause] + , + [Independent Clause 1] + , and/but + [Independent Clause 2]
While car sales dropped, truck sales rose, and bus sales remained stable.
⚠️ The "Spaghetti Sentence" Trap NEVER EXCEED 3 CLAUSES IN A SINGLE SENTENCE (1 Independent + 2 Dependent = Maximum)
❌ While car sales dropped, truck sales rose to 50,000, and bus sales remained stable, which was a surprise because fuel prices were rising. (5 clauses = Confusing!)
✅ While car sales dropped, truck sales rose to 50,000, and bus sales remained stable. (3 clauses = Clear and academic)

5. Super Sentences (Multi-Clause Chains)

5.1 What is a Super Sentence?

A super sentence is an advanced multi-clause structure that combines participle openings, relative clauses, main clauses, and participle results into a single, flowing sentence. This is a Band 8.5–9 technique.

5.2 The Super Sentence Template

Template:
[Participle Opening] + [Subject] + , [Relative Clause] , + [Main Verb Phrase] + [Participle of Consequence] + before + [Time Closing]

5.3 Super Sentence Anatomy

Example 1: Line Graph Starting at a modest 15% in 2000, the percentage of households with broadband, which initially lagged behind dial-up, surged dramatically over the next decade, overtaking its rival in 2006 before peaking at 95% in 2020.

Anatomy:
Participle Opening: Starting at a modest 15% in 2000
Subject: the percentage of households with broadband
Embedded Relative: , which initially lagged behind dial-up,
Main Verb Phrase: surged dramatically over the next decade
Participle of Consequence: , overtaking its rival in 2006
Time Closing: before peaking at 95% in 2020.
Example 2: Process Diagram After being harvested by hand, the coffee beans, which are still in their red outer layer, are transported to a processing plant where they are washed, fermented, and eventually dried under the sun.
Example 3: Map Description Having undergone significant redevelopment, the city center, which was once dominated by industrial buildings, now boasts modern shopping facilities, attracting tourists from around the world since the completion of the project in 2015.

5.4 Three Core Multi-Clause Patterns

PatternFormulaExample
Trend-Action Chain[Main Clause] + , + [Participle (-ing)] + before + [Time Clause]Organization D experienced fluctuations, increasing to 0.6 billion before falling to 0.3 billion.
Contrast & AppendWhile + [Dependent Clause] + , + [Main Clause] + , which + [Relative Clause]While domestic flights declined, international flights surged, which was the highest figure.
Process FlowOnce/After + [Dependent Clause] + , + [Main Clause] + , [Verb-ing]Once sorted by hand, they are placed on a conveyor belt, moving into the crusher.
[Participle Opening] + [Subject] + , [Relative Clause], + [Main Verb] + [Participle Result] + [Time Closing]
Starting at 15% broadband usage , which lagged behind, surged , overtaking dial-up before peaking at 95%.
📌 When to Use Super Sentences
  • Use 1–2 super sentences in your essay for maximum impact
  • Ideal for describing complex trends or processes
  • Shows the examiner a high level of grammatical control
  • Don't force them — they should flow naturally

💡 Key Takeaway

Examiners are not looking for the longest sentences possible. They are looking for variety, accuracy, clarity, and precision. Use a mix of all sentence types to demonstrate your grammatical range.

🎯 Conclusion: Master Sentence Structures

Key Principles:

  1. Variety is key — Mix simple, compound, complex, and super sentences
  2. Accuracy over complexity — Correct simple sentences are better than incorrect complex ones
  3. Clarity matters — Every sentence should be easy to read and understand
  4. Control your clauses — Never exceed 3 clauses in a single sentence

Target Counts:

  • Simple Sentences: 2–3 per essay
  • Compound Sentences: 2–3 per essay
  • Complex Sentences: 4–6 per essay
  • Compound-Complex: 0–2 per essay
  • Super Sentences: 0–1 per essay (Band 8+)
